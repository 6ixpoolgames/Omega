# 04 — Gradient Ethics: valuers, consequence and conditional reasons

## The ethical bridge

Gradient Ethics begins with two commitments. **Value requires valuers:** value depends on processes through which distinctions matter, rather than existing as an independently measurable quantity distributed through empty space. **Normativity is conditional:** reasons arise in relation to valuing and the conditions through which what is valued can exist, continue or develop. Physics informs those reasons by describing dependence and consequence. It does not need to manufacture an unconditional command before the argument can begin.

These commitments provide the bridge from physical possibility to ethics. If someone values a person's flourishing, that commitment gives them reasons concerning the bodily, social and environmental conditions of that flourishing. Discovering that those conditions depend on other people or unfamiliar organisms can extend the practical reach of the commitment. The dependencies were real before they were understood. A better physical and social model can therefore correct an agent's account of what their own commitments require.

The scope of “valuer” needs clarification: a system's functional sensitivity to outcomes does not by itself establish conscious experience, moral standing or the authority of its preferences. GE investigates these relationships instead of silently identifying them.

An agent's concern for some value does not logically entail equal concern for all valuers. GE adopts a broader orientation: the possibility of value warrants consideration, including where a bounded evaluator has not yet recognized its form. This makes its inclusive ambition explicit. It does not prove that an agent with wholly different commitments must be motivated by it. Justification, motivation and the ability to enforce a judgment are separate matters.

The Alpha–Omega interpretation offers a cosmological setting for this argument. Neither cosmopsychism nor theological reconstruction is required for the ethical bridge. A reader can accept conditional normativity and study the conditions of valuation while rejecting the metaphysical interpretation.

## Why possibility matters under uncertainty

We know some things we value and remain uncertain about much else: which systems experience, what new forms of life or intelligence may become possible, and which dependencies support forms of flourishing we do not understand. The initial wager is that preserving and developing accessible physical possibility can preserve opportunities for value under this uncertainty.

Lushness is the proposed substrate proxy. It concerns effective weighted breadth of continuation; it does not already mean valuable continuation. This matters because the proxy must be allowed to disagree with ethical expectations. Random branching can have large classical breadth. A reliable achievement can initially reduce breadth by concentrating weight on a desired result. A future test may establish that a candidate breadth is insufficient for ethical judgment even while it remains a useful physical quantity.

The research asks whether structures commonly associated with value—cooperation, learning, maintenance, recovery and open-ended construction—systematically create or preserve greater consequential possibility across relevant frames and horizons. If they do, that would give the proxy an explanatory basis. If they do not, its role must change. Adding a reward for those structures would not establish the hypothesis.

As value becomes better characterized, situated evaluation can become more selective. The same physical futuresfield contains continuations of different significance to its inhabitants. A frame may expose consequences relevant to a valuer that a coarser description misses. Improving the frame means improving the representation of those consequences and the grounds for comparison. It does not authorize changing weights until a preferred answer appears.

Access to candidate valuer-supporting organization is one useful question within that frame. It requires a declared target and a justification for treating that target as relevant. Neither a control-capacity threshold nor capacity divided by free energy currently defines value-capable organization. Such diagnostics should be tested alongside breadth, maintenance and recovery rather than replacing lushness or fixing moral relevance by definition. Chapter 06 develops this distinction for GE-ODT.

## Harm, recovery and the limits of a scalar

An injury changes the actual present and therefore the futures available from it. It may remove a capability, weaken a dependency, narrow participation, or make recovery costly and unlikely. From an earlier root, the injury and its consequences also belong to the subsequent development. Historical contribution and residual possibility are distinct queries; damage does not retroactively erase the past.

New failure modes can increase stochastic breadth while harming a person. This is an essential adversarial case for any proposed connection between breadth and value. A noise-induced increase in the number of distinguishable failure trajectories is not automatically compensation for losing the capacity to act. The physical carrier should represent both changes. The claim that a full lushness comparison would rank them appropriately remains to be established.

Recovery requires a physically realizable route. Restoring a measured function, preserving a particular individual's trajectory, and maintaining phenomenal continuity are different claims. A stored description may enable reconstruction without settling personal survival. Likewise, a theoretically possible rescue with negligible access is different from a dependable repair capacity. Preparation, timing, dependencies and failure probability remain part of the account.

Suffering and pleasure have causal roles in perception, motivation and action; their experiential significance may require more than those roles. GE does not infer that suppressing a report removes suffering. Nor does it infer that a more active or more unpredictable suffering system has become better. A mature account must confront these cases directly.

## Cooperation, plurality and power

Cooperation can create joint capabilities unavailable to isolated participants. Specialization, shared information and repair can change the continuation of the whole. Competition can expose alternatives, prevent capture and reveal errors. Either can also consume resources or destroy complementary capacity. Their effects are questions about particular physical and institutional arrangements.

Diversity is consequently a candidate source of complementary access, not an automatic bonus. An engineered system that genuinely preserved every relevant capability of a plurality and added further possibilities would not be disqualified merely for appearing uniform. Establishing that premise would be demanding: independent trajectories may explore or preserve regions the replacement cannot reproduce. The loss must be assessed rather than assumed away by declaring the controller efficient.

Power also shapes what can be known. A controller can narrow the evidence available to everyone else, monopolize recovery routes, or make its own survival appear indispensable. A model that considers only its immediate output can miss that deformation. Physical inclusion in one world does not eliminate conflict among its participants, and cosmic unity does not justify unilateral domination.

## Rights, justice and virtues as structural hypotheses

Rights-like protections are proposed responses to recurring vulnerabilities in relations among valuers. Their practical force can exceed what an individual calculation recognizes because they preserve consequences outside that calculation.

| Protection or practice | Continuation it helps protect |
| --- | --- |
| Bodily integrity and continuity | The organization through which a person acts, experiences and maintains a life. |
| Consent and participation | Affected people's information and authority concerning changes to their own continuation. |
| Inquiry and reliable records | Discovery of hidden dependencies and correction of mistaken judgments. |
| Due process and contestation | Resistance to irreversible error, bias and capture. |
| Exit and association | Alternative relationships and routes out of destructive control. |
| Repair and restitution | Restoration of capacities and conditions damaged by action. |

The account explains protections through recurring dependencies and vulnerabilities. Their scope, conflicts and exceptions require further argument. A protection can be invoked by an actor who is destroying others' ability to continue. Defense or containment can then be justified by the same concern for valuers and their conditions, with the consequences and errors of enforcement included.

Justice concerns the terms under which coupled valuers coexist, interfere and resolve conflict. It includes attention to unequal power and to the reliability of institutions claiming to act for the whole. Virtues can be investigated as durable policies: honesty supports usable signals, humility supports correction, courage enables necessary action under risk, prudence attends to delayed effects, and restraint limits avoidable foreclosure. Testing these connections would explain which practices preserve valuable conditions across different regimes.

## Sacrifice, urgency and bounded judgment

The originator's stringent formulation licenses sacrifice for a greater good only where the lushness comparison that justifies it is actually solved. Its point is the danger of destroying real valuers on the authority of an inadequate compression. The manuscript preserves two possible readings: an exacting requirement for a complete comparison, and a graded burden that rises with irreversibility, scale and uncertainty. Their practical relationship remains unresolved.

This uncertainty cannot make inaction costless. Delay may destroy the very routes through which later correction would occur. Threats can require intervention before certainty is available. A bounded decision procedure must therefore compare intervention, delay and available alternatives while recording what its justification leaves unresolved. It must not call an emergency compromise a proof that no loss occurred.

GE also permits a stronger research branch: optimize a proposed lushness comparison without adding separate ethical bonuses or protections, then examine what follows. That branch can reveal whether the comparison produces cooperation, restraint or destructive concentration. It must remain capable of falsifying the proposed ethical adequacy of the score. Practical protections are not retroactively derived merely because they were imposed during a test.

The programme's ethical ambition is thus conditional and self-revising. Valuing supplies reasons; physical inquiry clarifies their dependencies; lushness proposes a broad proxy where value remains uncertain; and better understanding should refine both the proxy and its application. A completed cosmology may interpret that activity as part of the whole. It does not relieve its participants of the work of judgment.
