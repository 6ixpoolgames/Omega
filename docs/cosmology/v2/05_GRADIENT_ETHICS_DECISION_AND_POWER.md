# 05 — Gradient Ethics, Decision, and Power

## 1. The ethical proposal

Gradient Ethics begins with value's dependence on valuers and asks what action does to the conditions under which valuation can persist and develop. Its central concern is the irreversible foreclosure of value-capable continuation, especially when an agent acts through a description too thin to register what it destroys.

The residual normative commitment is explicit: the continuation and development of affected valuers supply reasons for action. Their relevance cannot legitimately be removed merely by changing a description, manipulating a proxy, or declaring an unverified substitute equivalent.

This is a proposed moral commitment. The descriptive facts of persistence and deformation do not entail it on their own. Hume's discussion of transitions from “is” to “ought” marks the inferential boundary. Omega's strategy is to expose that boundary, then make the structural consequences of accepting the commitment as rigorous as possible. [Hume, *A Treatise of Human Nature*, III.i.1](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/3/1/1)

“Value requires valuers” establishes a dependence claim within the framework. It does not immediately establish that every particular valuer must survive, that all its preferences deserve satisfaction, or that reproduction is always obligatory. Those conclusions require further judgments about standing, conflict, substitution, and joint continuation.

## 2. Structural justification and internal motivation

A policy has consequences whether its actor is compassionate, ambitious, confused, or indifferent. Given an adequate model and explicit normative commitments, those consequences can support a judgment independently of the actor's self-description.

Motivation nevertheless affects which policy is selected and how it is sustained. Likewise, suffering can change attention, learning, cooperation, and capability. Internal signals matter through their place in the full dynamics even when they are not treated as the primary objective.

The ambition that one could inspect the structure and see who is right is therefore conditional: the relevant facts, standing, and comparison principles must be available. Structural inspection can refute a claimed preservation or compensation. It cannot choose the normative premises merely by displaying more detail.

## 3. Irreversibility and the significance of horizons

An action can foreclose a continuation that no available repair restores. Operationally, that claim depends on a fact language, allowed repairs, resources, uncertainty, and horizon. A broader repair budget can change the verdict. A longer horizon can reveal damage missed by a shorter one or recovery invisible within it.

Thermodynamic irreversibility, logical erasure, and inability to recover a declared fact are distinct notions. They can interact, but one should not be substituted for another without a physical model.

Irreversibility gives consequence persistence, not moral sign. Sacrifice can destroy one local continuation while preserving a richer joint organization. A proliferating cancer can preserve a local lineage while destroying the organism on which many coordinated processes depend. Those patterns motivate analysis at more than one boundary.

The horizon problem is established ground in this project. A new finite calculation displaying it would clarify the issue; it would not by itself advance the theory. The more demanding task is to justify which continuation facts and horizons should govern real decisions.

## 4. Rights and virtues as durable policies

Rights can function as protections against decisions made with short horizons, incomplete descriptions, or overwhelming local incentives. Their rationale within GE is that some forms of foreclosure destroy the conditions for future correction, participation, and valuation. The right protects a structure whose full significance may exceed the current evaluator's model.

This supports a family of rights arguments, not a deduction of a complete rights code. Determining bearers, scope, conflicts, and exceptions requires substantive normative work. An alleged global benefit cannot settle those questions by assuming away the affected parties' standing.

Virtues can be interpreted as policies that tend to preserve or expand valuable continuation across recurring contingencies. Prudence attends to delayed effects and uncertainty. Humility preserves routes of correction. Courage and capable restraint make preservation enforceable. Justice concerns the distribution and defense of the conditions under which valuers can continue together.

Consequential evaluation, rights, and virtue thus occupy different levels of one decision problem: evaluating consequences, stabilizing protective constraints, and cultivating reliable action under limited cognition. This is a reconstruction rather than a claim to absorb the full traditions of Mill, Kant, or Aristotle. Jonas is a more direct neighbor for responsibility toward the conditions of future life under expanded technological power. [Jonas, *The Imperative of Responsibility*](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Imperative_of_Responsibility.html?id=sRP3uJkxydQC)

## 5. Viability before optimization

The operational question is first whether a policy keeps declared continuation requirements achievable under the modeled disturbances. Viability theory provides the mathematical foundation for this question. [Aubin, *Viability Theory*](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-0-8176-4910-4)

The cosmological corridor between dissolution and sterile closure motivates what those requirements might concern. A computed viability kernel preserves only the supplied requirements. It cannot certify that the chosen register exhausts valuerhood or lushness.

Optimization can then occur within, or help refine, a justified corridor. GE need not prohibit optimization in principle. Its objection is to treating an incomplete objective as license to destroy the conditions under which error could be discovered and corrected.

Preserving every option is neither possible nor necessarily desirable. Meaningful action commits resources and excludes alternatives. The task is to discriminate generative commitment from damaging closure, including cases where commitment is necessary to create capabilities that no uncommitted policy can attain.

## 6. Omega Decision Theory

Omega Decision Theory is the project's current name for a staged decision architecture:

| Layer | Role | What it does not supply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ODT0 | Check that justification is soundly represented and that a proposed action preserves the declared corridor. | The moral legitimacy of the requirements or the right to impose them. |
| ODT1 | Compare licensed outcomes under a specified ordering, retaining unresolved comparisons. | A uniquely correct universal ordering of value. |
| ODT2 | Apply explicitly supplied arbitration information when a finite frontier remains. | Authority merely because an arbitration rule returns an answer. |

This structure can retain uncertainty, incomparability, and the absence of a licensed action. It is not yet a complete general decision theory with established treatment of logical dependence, acausal interaction, or every issue addressed by other decision-theoretic programs.

Its strongest contribution is conditional discipline: a policy claiming to preserve something must preserve it under the stipulated semantics, and a justification in an abstract model must remain valid in the concrete system to which it is applied.

## 7. Power, plurality, and containment

A competent plurality can pool resources, information, experimentation, and enforcement. Its aggregate resources may outweigh those of a defector, but this depends on the starting distribution, coordination costs, timing, and available opportunities. There is no theorem that plurality has more effective power simply because it is plural.

The project previously explored singleton fragility as a balancing mechanism. V2 treats it as one regime in a larger phase space. When asymmetry is manageable, environmental complexity and collective response may expose an excisive controller's weaknesses. Under severe asymmetry, the controller may neutralize those pressures or prevail before they matter.

The ethical fallback is a conditional argument for defensive containment by those whose continuation is threatened. It does not require demonstrating that the threatening agent secretly shares their values. It does require establishing the threat, the standing of the affected valuers, and a defensible relationship between the constraint imposed and the continuation it protects.

Containment is not automatically possible, and successful enforcement does not confer moral authority. The analogy to a physical singularity describes breakdown of a balancing regime; it is not a gravitational prediction.

## 8. The positive offer to AI development

The proposed benefit is sustained capability under novelty. Systems that retain correction, usable variety, and the capacity to revise their own models may reach useful solutions that premature commitment or destructive proxy optimization would make inaccessible.

For a laboratory, this suggests preserving reusable experimental infrastructure, maintaining genuinely different hypotheses long enough to discriminate them, and building agents that can recognize when their current representation no longer covers the task. These can support discovery and adaptation as well as constrain failure.

The incentive remains conditional. Maintaining options has costs; specialization can win; a short race can reward a policy that is poor over a longer horizon. GE offers a hypothesis about durable capability, not a demonstrated competitive advantage for every deployment.

A concrete demonstration would compare matched agents on tasks where actions alter later capabilities. The relevant outputs would include achieved performance, adaptation to changed conditions, and whether failures remain recoverable. A favorable result would support that tested capability claim, not prove a universal ethics. The Off-Switch Game is an adjacent example of incentives for retaining correction under uncertainty, under its own assumptions. [Hadfield-Menell and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.08219)

## 9. What can bind a more capable reasoner

GE seeks arguments that remain coherent when an agent understands more than their authors. If the agent accepts the relevance of valuer continuation, it should not be able to evade a valid structural constraint through renaming, unsound compression, or fabricated recovery. Better knowledge may overturn a particular approximation while preserving the underlying demand for accurate justification.

An orthogonal agent may reject the normative commitment altogether. Argument alone does not guarantee motivation. Human adoption therefore matters: it can shape the objectives, institutions, training material, and early conditions under which more powerful systems develop.

The practical purpose is to make the conditional argument clear enough to evaluate and robust enough to survive changes in model and scale. Cosmology can explain its place in a larger vision, but it is not a prerequisite for that argument.
