# 06 — Theology and Divine Completion

## 1. What this reconstruction attempts

The theological branch asks how a completed, internally differentiated reality could support concepts such as unity, creation, eternity, providence, communion, and judgment. It also asks where those reconstructions stop.

The method begins with metaphysical commitments and investigates their entailments. It does not infer a personal God merely because a mathematical object is complete, nor claim that physics has measured the ultimate ground of existence.

The originating image is Wolframian in its relational unity and block-Everettian in its completion. One reality contains many situated organizations through its internal structure. The question is how far that picture can carry inherited theological language without making the language promise more than the picture establishes.

## 2. Formal description, manifestation, and ultimate reality

Three distinctions are useful. **Formal description** is the structure expressed by a model. **Manifest reality** is the actuality that the description concerns, including phenomenal occurrence if that is not exhausted by the model. **Ultimate reality** is whatever finally constitutes or grounds that actuality.

These are distinctions in the inquiry. They do not posit three substances or three spatially separate worlds. Formal closure could make an adequate formal account exhaustive of actuality. A dual-aspect or other account could preserve a distinction without adding an external realm.

For continuity with the earlier theology, $G^*$ denotes God-as-such or the ultimate ground named under apophatic restraint; $D_\alpha$ denotes actuality manifest under a specified grammar; and $\mathfrak F(D_\alpha)$ denotes its formal description. The notation does not establish that $G^*$ is distinct from the whole it grounds, personal, conscious, or necessary.

The central epistemic asymmetry is straightforward. We encounter situated manifestation and build descriptions from within it. We do not acquire an external vantage merely by quantifying over a completed model.

## 3. The veil and the approach toward a summa

The **apophatic veil** marks the limit of justified positive description. It should be distinguished from Rawls's ethical use of “veil of ignorance.” Here it concerns what finite, situated inquiry can establish about reality and its ground.

Some ignorance is ordinary and potentially reducible: unknown laws, incomplete models, limited observations. A deeper underdetermination arises when rival accounts agree on every accessible consequence. No empirical test available under those conditions can distinguish them.

An alleged reality beyond physics is not automatically protected from investigation. If a proposal specifies detectable effects, it makes empirical commitments. If it specifies none, its assessment proceeds through consistency, explanatory adequacy, and metaphysical argument rather than a purported measurement beyond all measurement.

The project narrows the veil by identifying what an adequate account must accommodate: the existence of awareness, stable relations, situated access, internal consequence, and whatever further facts investigation establishes. It can also expose contradictions in particular reconstructions. It cannot conclude that the remaining compatible account is unique unless that uniqueness is demonstrated.

Thus the framework approaches a summa in breadth and coherence while leaving ultimate exhaustiveness unclaimed. This is not a formal convergence theorem. More formal precision can clarify the boundary without abolishing it.

Pseudo-Dionysius is a central neighbor for the discipline of affirming and withholding theological predicates. Omega gives that discipline a contemporary role at the interface between formal description, phenomenal actuality, and ultimate explanation. [Pseudo-Dionysius, *Mystical Theology* and *Divine Names*](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/rolt/dionysius.html)

## 4. Unity, simplicity, and aseity

One fundamental reality can be internally differentiated without being assembled from ontologically independent ingredients. Under this reading, “atoms of space” are positions within relational organization. Mathematical decomposition does not itself settle the direction of metaphysical dependence.

This supports a reconstruction of simplicity as fundamental unity. Strict classical divine simplicity makes a stronger claim: no real composition of parts, subject and attributes, essence and existence, or other metaphysical constituents. A relationally differentiated whole need not satisfy that criterion. Aquinas's doctrine is therefore a precise comparison point rather than a standard silently adopted and then declared fulfilled. [Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae*, I, question 3](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1003.htm)

**Aseity** concerns independence of existence from anything else. It is not self-causation. **Necessity** concerns whether the reality could fail to exist. Neither follows just from there being one kind of substance.

The preferred commitment is stronger than homogeneity: one fundamental reality whose differentiation and consequences are intrinsic, requiring no external ground or operator. That is a coherent candidate for aseity. Its independence is a metaphysical commitment whose justification remains under investigation.

Wolfram's all-rules argument supports a nearby move by treating consequences as belonging to formal structure without an external executor. The transition from formal inevitability to actual existence is the remaining philosophical step. Omega leaves open whether that step can be completed, including for awareness. [Wolfram, *Why Does the Universe Exist?*](https://writings.stephenwolfram.com/2021/04/why-does-the-universe-exist-some-perspectives-from-our-physics-project/)

Spinoza provides a major neighbor for substance monism; Plotinus for the relation between unity and multiplicity. Their accounts differ from one another and from this proposal. They locate the question without supplying a shared doctrine we can inherit wholesale. [Spinoza, *Ethics*, Part I](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800); [Plotinus, *Enneads*, especially V](https://classics.mit.edu/Plotinus/enneads.html)

## 5. Logos and creation through perspective

**Logos** is used here for intelligible articulation: the relations under which determinate structure becomes describable. The provisional grammar of distinction, relation, and orientation identifies what our formal representations repeatedly require. It is not an established unique grammar of all being.

This usage acknowledges the Johannine Logos and the philosophical tradition around it. Identifying the technical articulation with a divine person would require an additional theological argument. The packet does not derive Trinitarian doctrine from a tuple of primitives.

At the creaturely level, perspective participates in individuation. A boundary organizes exchanges, distinguishes internal from external relations, and can support a continuing locus of action and awareness. Creation in this reconstructive sense is the emergence or specification of a finite organization within the whole.

Such individuation is **non-excisive** when it creates real differentiation without treating the creature as a severed fragment of ultimate substance. The boundary can preserve dependence and exchange. This is compatible with the broader intuition that unity expresses itself through plurality.

The analogy is adjacent to hylomorphic and emanationist accounts but proves neither. It also does not establish creation ex nihilo in the classical sense. The correspondence concerns how finite organization can be real within an encompassing unity.

## 6. Eternity, knowledge, power, and providence

Eternity receives the clearest reconstruction. A completed whole does not successively acquire its internal history. Its inhabitants nevertheless encounter different times from different situations. Aquinas's treatment of eternity is a relevant comparison, while the block model supplies a different route to non-successive totality. [Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae*, I, question 10](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1010.htm)

Other attributes require further bridges:

| Attribute | Structural reconstruction | Further question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knowledge | The whole contains the relations and records of its history. | Is there a subject that knows them? Containment is not cognition. |
| Power | All physically admissible capacities and consequences belong to the specified reality. | Does one agent command them? Possibility is not control. |
| Providence | Local actions occupy an intelligible completed order. | Is the order intentional, beneficent, or directed toward a good? |
| Pure actuality | The whole does not wait externally for its internal events to become present. | Is this equivalent to the classical elimination of potency? That is not established. |

The distinctions prevent formal completion from being mistaken for a proof of the divine attributes. They also identify where a stronger theological account would have to contribute something.

## 7. Awareness and divine participation

Universal habitation proposes awareness at every qualifying perspective. It does not, without an integration principle, establish that all those occurrences belong to one phenomenal subject.

A stronger theological reading proposes that awareness is a divine property expressed through finite perspectives, perhaps one awareness differently situated throughout reality. Under that reading, divinity participates in the suffering as well as the flourishing of creatures. This changes the picture of an entirely insulated creator imposing costs on others.

It does not by itself answer the problem of evil. Participation in suffering does not establish that the suffering is justified, necessary, or preferable to alternatives. Nor does the hypothesis supply a physical test unless the proposed correspondence entails distinguishable effects.

The theological speculation is therefore compatible with the preferred habitation picture, while remaining more specific than it. The framework can retain the possibility without making GE depend on it.

## 8. Communion and generative unity

Communion is reconstructed as integration that sustains and enriches its participants' value-capable continuation. Coordination can reduce destructive friction while preserving meaningful differentiation, correction, and further generation.

The limiting image of laminar interaction is useful here. A maximally integrated plurality may implement the same effective organization as a singleton containing the requisite internal variety. Whether an isomorphism exists depends on the relations being preserved, including any eventually established phenomenal facts.

Terminal solitude belongs to the opposing tendency: unity secured through excision and loss of regenerative capacity. The moral distinction is not simply one versus many. A noisy plurality can be destructive; an integrated unity can be generative.

Love can be interpreted as active concern for another's continuation and development within a shared world. Justice concerns the terms under which such continuation is protected and coordinated. Capable restraint gives these commitments practical force. These are proposed structural readings of virtues, not reductions of every religious use of those terms.

## 9. Judgment, redemption, heaven, and hell

Actions leave consequences in the continuation available to the actor and others. Judgment can be reconstructed as assessment of that structure; it need not initially mean an externally imposed penalty.

Redemption names the restoration of generative capability after damaging contraction. A recovery route can require changes in organization, policy, or relations. Before a genuine loss of recoverability, a process can reopen a future it had been closing.

Heaven is an image of sustained generative communion. Hell is an image of persistent closure or degradation beyond accessible recovery. These images help articulate the framework's limits but do not establish postmortem destinations, cosmic punishment, or a literal quantum-immortality mechanism.

In particular, the existence of some continuation in a quantum description does not guarantee a person's experienced survival or its quality. The framework acknowledges the whole supplied structure while asking what remains accessible and generative from a particular situation.

## 10. The unfinished theological claim

Omega offers an intelligible route from relational unity to situated multiplicity, and from situated multiplicity to questions of awareness, value, and generative integration. It supports conditional reconstructions of theological language and exposes the additional commitments each requires.

The unresolved questions are substantive: whether formal structure exhausts actuality; whether the whole is aseitic or necessary; how awareness corresponds to perspective; whether local awareness integrates into divine subjecthood; and whether generative richness supplies a defensible account of goodness.

The veil is not an excuse to answer those questions arbitrarily. It identifies where an account must remain a commitment, conjecture, or open question until a stronger argument becomes available.
