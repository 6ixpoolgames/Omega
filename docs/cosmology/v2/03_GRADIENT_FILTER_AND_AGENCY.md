# 03 — Gradient, Filter, and Agency

## 1. Gradients and conditions of continuation

Many processes continue by exploiting differences: available energy, chemical potential, information, resources, or opportunities for action. Omega uses **gradient** broadly for an oriented difference that can shape continuation. A literal gradient requires a specified function and geometry; the general term does not imply that every domain already has them.

A **filter** consists of conditions under which a process, lineage, or organization remains represented in later situations. Filtering need not be deliberate. Some arrangements dissolve, some reproduce, some stabilize, and some reorganize their surroundings to become more durable.

The central dynamic intuition is recursive. What continues can change the conditions of its own further continuation. What replicates can occupy more of the available substrate. What learns can change which perturbations it can survive. Agency emerges within this interaction between differentiation and persistence, rather than being introduced as an unexplained chooser outside the world.

## 2. Cosmic Darwinism as an engine, not a guarantee

Darwinian selection supplies the principal neighbor: variation, differential continuation or reproduction, and inheritance can change the composition of a population. Omega explores how far that pattern generalizes across physical and organizational scales. [Darwin, *On the Origin of Species*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1228)

For a simple replicating population, a model might write

$$
\mathbb E[n_i(t+1)\mid n(t)]=n_i(t)\,w_i(n(t),e(t)),
$$

where $n_i$ counts instances of type $i$, $e$ describes the environment, and $w_i$ is an effective growth factor including survival and reproduction. Heritable variation changes the types available; interactions make their growth factors depend on population composition. This equation illustrates the filter without identifying fitness with value.

Valuers need not logically prefer their own survival. However, valuers whose organization supports continued valuing can become more represented among later valuers, other things being equal. Reproduction can amplify that effect. This is the dynamic pressure the framework takes seriously.

The qualifications are part of the engine. A sterile replicator can expand rapidly. A generous organization can be exploited. An adapted system can disappear when the environment changes. Selection can operate at several conflicting levels. A persistence filter favors what passes the operative filter, not whatever an observer independently calls rich, good, or wise.

Replication is especially relevant to occupying expanding opportunities through descendants. It is not a logical requirement for every persistent process: one organization can grow, transform, or indefinitely maintain itself without making separate copies. The proper unit may be a lineage, institution, distributed process, or changing pattern rather than an individual organism.

## 3. Deformers and the endogenous board

A **deformer** is a process whose presence or organization changes the continuation available in a surrounding system. Rivers, organisms, institutions, tools, and optimizers can all deform possibilities. Large causal effect alone does not establish agency or standing.

In a matched model, replace process $D$ with a relevant alternative $D'$. If the resulting continuation structures differ, that comparison identifies an effect attributable to the replacement. What counts as a relevant alternative depends on the causal question.

Deformation can change reachability, resource availability, correlations, repair routes, bottlenecks, or the stability of a pattern. It can also change the effective action space. An invention may create an action that was previously unavailable; an institution may enable coordinated action; a destructive policy may remove the infrastructure needed to recover from it.

The geometry is therefore endogenous at the effective level. This need not mean that fundamental physical laws change. Under fixed laws, organizations can create new capabilities and new useful coordinates for describing continuation.

The analogy with matter shaping spacetime motivates this reciprocal relationship between inhabitants and their field. It does not supply an Einstein equation for ethics or establish a literal futures field in physics.

## 4. The transition into agency

Agency appears when a deformer has endogenous, feedback-mediated organization through which its internal state or policy systematically changes continuation. Stronger agency can sustain, revise, or expand that control channel.

Earlier Gradient Ethics language called this a first phase transition into agency. In v2, “phase transition” identifies a proposed qualitative change in organization. A literal transition would require a control parameter, an order parameter, and evidence of the relevant change. Those have not been supplied for agency in general.

A minimal schematic model is

$$
x_{t+1}=F(x_t,a_t,e_t),\qquad
a_t\sim\pi(\cdot\mid m_t),\qquad
m_{t+1}=G(m_t,o_{t+1},a_t),
$$

where $m_t$ is internal organization or memory, $o_t$ an input, and $a_t$ an action. To interpret this as agency rather than arbitrary notation, the model must establish the actual feedback relations and counterfactual effects of changing $\pi$ or $G$.

Self-modification extends the model to changes in policy, representation, or embodiment. The relevant challenge is continuity of effective correction through those changes. An agent that optimizes away the means to notice error may retain local performance while losing the capacity to respond to a changed world.

## 5. Vortical organization and multiscale integration

The vortex intuition concerns organized persistence through throughput. Parts change while a recurrent pattern remains. Effects circulate through a boundary and its environment, helping regenerate the conditions under which the pattern continues.

Autopoiesis supplies a biological and organizational neighbor: the network that produces and maintains a living unity is central to the account. Omega uses this to motivate endogenous maintenance while keeping its proposed agency class broader and its valuer class unresolved. [Varela, Maturana, and Uribe, *Autopoiesis: The Organization of Living Systems, Its Characterization and a Model*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0303264774900318)

Levin's work offers a further neighbor in treating cognition and goal-directed organization across scales and embodiments. The relevance is methodological: test the range of goals, interventions, and coordinated competencies a system supports. Omega does not infer a cosmic mind or an immortal soul from biological control. [Levin, *Technological Approach to Mind Everywhere*](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnsys.2022.768201)

Integration can constrain components while expanding the capabilities of the whole. An organism's cells have fewer independent trajectories than an unconstrained collection, yet their coordination supports new kinds of continuation. The ethical question cannot be answered by treating every reduction of local freedom as net contraction.

Conversely, higher-level integration can conceal losses at lower levels. Which continuities matter, what is replaced, and what remains capable of correction must be examined across scales.

## 6. Ashby: capability through appropriate variety

Ashby's law of requisite variety is a primary foundation for thinking about regulation under disturbance. Effective regulation requires an appropriate range of responses relative to the distinctions in the disturbances and the outcomes being constrained. It is a relation among regulator, environment, and task. [Ashby, *An Introduction to Cybernetics*, chapter 11](https://ashby.info/Ashby-Introduction-to-Cybernetics.pdf)

This makes Ashby a useful starting point for distinguishing two controller strategies. One expands its models, responses, and internal differentiation to meet contingency. Another simplifies what it must face by suppressing environmental variation. Both can regulate successfully in a specified task. Their effects on the wider futures field may differ radically.

Raw internal complexity is an inadequate substitute for effective regulatory variety. Unused noise, ornamental simulations, and redundant copies need not enlarge the responses available to consequential disturbances. Nor does regulatory success establish ethical adequacy: a controller can become highly competent at an excisive objective.

The candidate comparison must therefore inspect usable responses, retained environmental differentiation, generative capacity, and the consequences of constraint for other valuers. No single Ashby-derived number currently settles all four.

## 7. What the dynamic hypothesis predicts conditionally

The framework expects generative, adaptable organization to have advantages where novel contingencies reward expanded response capability, useful variation can be retained, and interactions permit its benefits to accumulate. It expects excisive simplification to incur costs where suppressed variety later becomes necessary for survival or discovery.

Those conditions can fail. An environment may remain simple, a dominant controller may prevent the relevant disturbances, or an excisive replicator may exploit its initial advantage before competitors can respond. The board's starting conditions and the degree of asymmetry are therefore central variables.

The research target is a phase diagram of regimes, not a universal assurance that the generous policy wins. A useful model would identify when diversity, integration, selective simplification, or defensive constraint improves persistent generativity—and when it does not.
