# Frame information: history, accessible records, and forgetting

Date: 2026-09-29. Finite mathematical clarification and executable witness.
Parent: [acceptance protocol](frame_information_audit_protocol_v0.md).

## The distinction

The [present-frame note](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1S7WvGHXOn_Sa1JJfkKA7tRbOhVdwrYWW/view)
defines a frame using information generated by the record process up to the
present. Its erasure discussion, and the
[consequences note](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1IOvrMI3-GDz_sFippjECfFbk4RSfneNP/view),
also describe losing previously available records. An accumulated history and
currently accessible records are different information objects. This note
states a consistent finite interpretation without changing those Drive sources.

Fix a program and an input law, giving one probability space of complete runs.
Let O_t be the current local observation and M_t the current local memory,
immediately before the action at decision step t. Define:

```text
H_t = (observations through t, actions before t, memories through t)
A_t = (O_t, M_t)
F_t = sigma(H_t)        analyst's accumulated information
G_t = sigma(A_t)        information currently available to the controller
```

The F_t form an increasing filtration. Each A_t is determined by H_t, so
G_t is contained in F_t at that time. But G_t need not be contained in G_(t+1).
Losing all accessible copies of a fact can merge information cells.

The controller action is a function of O_t and M_t. A verifier retaining H_t,
or a predictor caching hidden world states, does not grant that information to
the controller. Any archive access must enter through a modeled observation
channel, with its physical delay and resource charge. The input identifier is
used for preparation and grading, not as an argument to the local policy.

Perfect recall here means G_t is contained in G_(t+1) on the positive-mass
support of the fixed experiment. It is an explicit property, not a consequence
of describing a perspective as a frame. A finite-memory controller may have
perfect recall for one finite experiment while lacking it for others.

## Which identities hold

For a fixed rational readout X on the finite run space, conditioning on either
H_t or A_t is well-defined on every positive-mass cell. An event measurable in
the chosen information has conditional probability zero or one. A past event
does not become measurable merely because it happened in the past.

Because F_t is contained in F_(t+1), the tower property gives:

```text
E[E[X | F_(t+1)] | F_t] = E[X | F_t].
```

Thus E[X | F_t] is a martingale under this one law. The same assertion for
G_t requires the corresponding nesting. Erasure can break it.

If a later accessible record is a coarsening of the earlier information, the
reverse conditioning identity still holds:

```text
E[E[X | G_t] | G_(t+1)] = E[X | G_(t+1)].
```

This is coherent loss of information, not inconsistent probability. Equality
of unconditional expectations at two times does not establish a martingale;
the relevant test conditions on each earlier information cell. Likewise a
constant readout satisfies the displayed forward identity even when nesting
fails. The implementation checks refinement separately and retains a pair of
outcomes witnessing any failed refinement.

All these statements are under a fixed program, common probability law, and
fixed readout. Conditioning on different intervention-dependent populations
or selecting a different program after seeing the hidden input changes the
question. Zero-probability information cells are not assigned posteriors.

## The erasure witness

A fair target bit is initially displayed. Before erasure, the local controller
memory retains it. After a mandatory reset, the observation and memory are
identical for bit 0 and bit 1. The historical observation remains in the
verification trace, but there is no channel from that trace to the actor.

For X equal to the bit, the historical posterior remains 0 or 1. The accessible
posterior after erasure is 1/2. Conditional on an earlier bit-0 frame, the next
accessible posterior has expected value 1/2 rather than 0; conditional on an
earlier bit-1 frame, it is 1/2 rather than 1. These are exact failures of the
forward martingale identity, with differences +1/2 and -1/2.

The reverse coarsening identity holds: averaging the old 0 and 1 estimates in
the blank frame gives 1/2. With memory preserved, the forward identity also
holds. With a permitted archive read, the accessible estimate becomes 0 or 1
again, and the refinement from the blank frame to the read frame obeys the
tower identity. Merely retaining a sealed archive does not reveal its content.

Operationally, any allowed guess after complete local erasure is independent
of the target bit. Its success under the fair input law is at most 1/2, attained
by either constant guess. With an accessible remembered or retrieved bit,
guessing that bit attains one. The installed catalogue includes these programs.

This is a witness of two different mistakes: treating accumulated records as
currently available, and treating physical existence of a record as permission
to read it. It is not a claim that information is destroyed everywhere in the
universe. The target remains in the grader's inaccessible state, and the archive
can remain physically present while unreadable.

## Apparatus and accounting

The declared phases are show, maintain/reset, access, optional read, guess,
and terminal. All actions before the reset are forced and identical across
inputs, so they cannot encode the bit into another local channel. Every
installed controller shares the observation and mandatory-update tables.

There are three memory states: blank, zero, and one. The finite catalogue is
the Cartesian product of two fixed access decisions (skip or retrieve) and
eight binary guess tables on those memory states. This catalogue is complete
for those declared choices, not for arbitrary programmable hardware or policies.

Every nonterminal transition costs one token, except the retrieval request,
which costs two. Skipping therefore finishes in four ticks at cost four.
Retrieving finishes in five ticks at cost six; denied or missing archive reads
cost the same. At horizon four, a retrieving run has spent five tokens and is
unfinished at the guess phase. At horizon five with budget five, its completed
cost six rejects the entire program. Programs and results are never selected
separately by input, and rejected or unfinished paths are not renormalized away.

Charges are declared tokens in this apparatus, not a derivation of the physical
energy cost of memory erasure or archive manufacture.

## Relationship to the optimal-value claim

The audit checks posterior coherence and fixed-program response laws. It does
not prove an optimal-control martingale theorem for arbitrary controller
catalogues. In a finite dynamic program, a Bellman value process has the usual
supermartingale property under an admissible policy, and equality under an
optimal policy, when information, continuation feasibility, and policy pasting
support that recursion. The deadline and remaining budget must be consistent.
Perfect recall helps provide the information filtration; it does not by itself
make an arbitrary finite catalogue closed under conditional policy choices.

An earlier optimal value must already account for a mandatory future erasure.
The posterior counterexample is not a claim that a correctly specified optimum
unexpectedly falls from one to one-half. Here the erased apparatus has optimal
completed-guess probability one-half from the start.

## Relationship to C1 and the project claims

The four existing predictors execute these cases without a semantic change.
Their retained histories and caches are internal computational or verification
objects. The controller interface determines what the actor can use. Comparing
all four methods checks this separation at the full-law level, not merely by
matching the final best score.

The C1/conventional-quotient equivalence remains intact. These are public
acceptance controls and an elementary mathematical clarification, not an
independent experiment, a new extraction result, a normative completion of
the lushness order, or a Lean-checked proof. Broader questions from the new
notes remain separate research tasks.
