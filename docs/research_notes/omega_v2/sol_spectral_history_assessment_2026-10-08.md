# Sol's history Gram effective-rank proposal: assessment

Good carrier description, valid fixed-family diagnostic, unsupported promotion
to intrinsic coherent possibility extent. This is the same spectral candidate
already explicitly tested in quantum_extent_v0 and quantum_readers_v0, not a
new untested formula. Do not reject merely because it differs from endpoint
breadth: the user's target remains full continuation. The decisive problems are
history-family dependence and what the spectral functional discards.

## Exact interpretation

For pure initial state, branch vectors v_a=C_a psi and D_ab=<v_b|v_a>, the
nonzero eigenvalues of D equal those of sigma=sum_a |v_a><v_a| (up to the
irrelevant transpose associated with this D convention). For exhaustive
projective histories, sigma is exactly the output of the sequence with unread
projective measurements at the chosen checkpoints. D retains off-diagonal
history entries, but its spectrum is this nonselective-measurement spectrum.
The actual unmeasured output is |sum_a v_a><sum_b v_b|, including cross terms.
Thus the spectrum describes a history-ensemble geometry relative to a family;
it does not automatically measure the recombined physical future.

Earlier counterexample: same unmarked phase-zero H-H circuit, endpoint-only
history family gives spectral breadth1; adding unmeasured intermediate Z
resolution gives2, with exactly the same physical unitary/output. A different
question/resolution may legitimately give a different diagnostic. It cannot
be called intrinsic to the circuit without specifying a physical family rule.
Same resolved interferometer at phases0/pi/2 has identical D eigenvalues but
output breadth1/2. Spectral functions discard phase relationships to the physical
grouping. Other Renyi effective ranks inherit this isospectral blindness.

For fixed branches, applying one global unitary U to every v_a preserves all
their inner products. Global recording or uncomputation therefore cannot by
itself make orthogonal full-system vectors coincide. The prior table4/2/1 is
three summaries with specified intermediate/final projectors, not a derivation
that universal history directions were physically compressed4->2->1.

## Normalization and subdivision

The tested exhaustive projective families already have TrD=1. In a more general
quantum-measure decomposition, normalization is sum_ab D_ab=1; TrD need not1.
Dividing by TrD is a choice of ensemble normalization, not a universal Born rule.

Tiny algebra check: v0=|0>/sqrt2, v1=|1>/sqrt2 gives spectral breadth2. Replace

candidate excludes it, that exclusion must be part of the history-index measure.
Permutations and isometric embeddings do preserve spectra. These different
meanings of subdivision must not be conflated.

## What survives

Classical diagonal limit and independent tensor-product multiplicativity are
exact. Rank-one normalized Gram matrices have effective rank1. Rerooting from
a sufficient full present plus law works, with the history-family specification
included. Monotonic increase under generic 'decoherence' or decrease under
generic 'uncomputation' is not established without specifying the map/partition.

The orbital comparison is useful: spatial participation volumes need a physical
position observable and reference measure. A pure orbital can occupy a large
spatial region while its density matrix has spectral effective rank1. This
supports relational geometry, not replacement of spatial extent by spectral
entropy. In history space, specifying the physical counterpart of position and
its reference structure remains consequential.

Recommended next question: can the physical local update/record algebra supply
a justified history family and transformation rules, so a spectral profile has

were a fresh candidate, or declare the partition problem solved by taking

correlation/dynamical entropy, not ordinary entropy of the actual pure universe.
