# Sol's pullback history volume: a restricted candidate

Status: assessment of the supplied proposal, with analytic counterexamples. No new simulation, physical law, metric, or lushness ordering adopted. No publication requested.

## Verdict

There is a genuine established construction here: intrinsic volume of a smooth, finite-dimensional family of physical histories under a specified Riemannian history metric. Unlike adding residual annotations to a list of probabilities, this construction can make geometry enter the extent calculation. It is worth retaining as a restricted candidate.

The proposal does not establish that the current stochastic chemistry has such a history manifold. Its claims about redundant parameters, reconvergence, and a general volume element need correction before implementation. These are concrete mathematical issues, not objections to the programme's motivating assumptions.

The existing frame construction still supplies native conditional probabilities. A physical metric/reference volume is an additional ingredient; no value prior or preferred chooser is needed. All consequential thermal and noisy histories remain represented.

## What the smooth construction actually supplies

Let a physically specified finite-dimensional parameter domain Xi carry its native conditional probability nu. Suppose F maps it smoothly and injectively into physical histories, with full rank d and specified local configuration metric G. The L2 history tangent metric pulls back to

    h_T(xi) = integral_0^T J_t(xi)^T G(F_t(xi)) J_t(xi) dt.

Then sqrt(det h_T) dxi is the induced d-dimensional volume element. It is invariant under smooth changes of parameter coordinates when all factors transform together. It is not invariant under changing the physical metric, temporal resolution, or observation map.

Under these conditions P = F_*nu is the physical history law. If P is absolutely continuous with respect to the intrinsic image volume mu, Sol's effective-volume formula is meaningful:

    V_q = [integral (dP/dmu)^q dmu]^(1/(1-q)).

For disjoint cells with volume v_i and native mass w_i, and constant density within each cell,

    V_q = [sum_i w_i^q v_i^(1-q)]^(1/(1-q)),
    V_1 = exp[-sum_i w_i log(w_i/v_i)].

Thus equal mass lists need not imply equal extent when cell volumes differ. Geometry now genuinely participates. Volume units require a common convention; scaling mu by c scales every V_q by c. Logarithms can be read relative to the common volume unit. Equal volume does not imply equal geometry, so the underlying relational object must still be retained.

## Redundant parameters are not automatically removed

If F(u,v) is the constant history with value u, its pullback is diag(T,0). The determinant integral over the two-parameter domain is zero, although the image has positive intrinsic one-dimensional length. The formula correctly returns zero two-dimensional area; it does not automatically recover the lower-dimensional extent.

Conversely, F(u) = (cos u, sin u), 0 <= u <= 4 pi, has Jacobian integral 4 pi but image length 2 pi. The integral counts parameter multiplicity. The standard area formula explicitly includes that multiplicity.

The repair is intrinsic image dimension, appropriate charts/strata, and multiplicity accounting. Integrating a pseudodeterminant over the original redundant domain alone is not a general repair. Identifying duplicate parameter descriptions must not identify physically distinct histories merely because they share an endpoint or residual continuation.

Constraints can also produce discrete pieces and intersecting strata rather than one smooth submanifold. Different intrinsic dimensions have different volume units and cannot be added without further physical scale choices.

## One jump already defeats the proposed smooth pullback

Take the binary history Gamma_tau(t) = 1[t >= tau], with unit distance between binary states. Under the proposed metric,

    D(Gamma_tau, Gamma_(tau+delta))^2 = |delta|.

Consequently,

    ||(Gamma_(tau+delta) - Gamma_tau)/delta||_L2^2 = 1/|delta|.

There is no finite L2 tangent derivative with respect to jump time. The proposed h is therefore unavailable even for this simplest continuous-time jump process.

This does not destroy the metric-space geometry. A D-ball of radius epsilon spans a jump-time interval of radius epsilon^2. The one-jump family has covering growth proportional to epsilon^(-2), and Hausdorff dimension two under this metric. Under the earlier absolute mismatch-time distance, that same family has dimension one. This is a consequence of taking a square root of distance, not a new physical direction generated by the process.

For the same discrete state distance, the two covering profiles are identical after reparameterizing radii. The square-root change does not fix the previous whole-state coupling collision. A different physically justified local/joint geometry could change that result, but remains to be specified.

A finite CTMC state space does not imply a finite-dimensional smooth path space. Histories range over event sequences and jump counts, with continuous ordered jump times. There can also be a positive no-jump atom. One positive-dimensional smooth reference volume does not dominate this entire probability law. Fixed-word timing sectors and their native masses can be retained as a profile; combining their different dimensions needs justification.

Finite-bandwidth physical records could smooth jumps, but that is an explicit observation model. For a unit step replaced by a linear ramp of duration eta, the jump-time tangent norm squared is 1/eta away from boundaries. The resulting length depends on eta. Numerical smoothing is not a physically derived volume element.

## Accumulated history does not contract when futures reconverge

For fixed Xi, fixed physical metric, and nested unnormalized time windows,

    h_(T2) - h_(T1) = integral_T1^T2 J_t^T G J_t dt >= 0.

On a fixed full-rank family, the determinant and volume are nondecreasing with horizon. Sol's negative accumulated-volume expansion rate does not follow from this construction. Reconvergence stops or slows the addition of distinguishable history; it does not remove the prefix.

For an exact smooth example, take Gamma_xi(t) = exp(-k t) xi, xi in [0,1], k > 0, with unit state metric. The instantaneous interval shrinks as exp(-k T), but the history image length is

    V_history(T) = sqrt[(1 - exp(-2 k T))/(2 k)],

which increases to a finite limit.

This supports the programme's preservation of consequential finite histories. Contraction of an instantaneous image, a moving-window comparison, or the residual field at a later cut is a different quantity. Horizon normalization also changes the comparison. Keep these distinctions explicit.

## An established mathematical foothold

For linear dynamics with history Gamma_xi(t) = C exp(A t) xi and Euclidean record geometry,

    h_T = integral_0^T exp(A^T t) C^T C exp(A t) dt.

This is exactly the finite-horizon observability Gramian. Here C represents a declared physical record/frame map, not an optimizing external experimenter. The identity gives a tractable analytic case and useful existing machinery.

It measures how initial physical variations become distinguishable through histories. Calling its expansion generativity would be premature: passive persistence and sensitive dynamics can enlarge it too. With fixed d independent smooth initial variations, the image rank cannot exceed d. Emergent access can still reorganize within that fixed underlying dimension.

## Coverings and continuum limits

Covering counts remain legitimate finite-resolution extent probes without any smooth-volume limit. Multiplying a minimum cover count by epsilon^d is not a general equality with intrinsic volume: dimension, regularity, ball geometry and covering efficiency matter. A dimension alone does not guarantee a finite nonzero limiting content.

Discretizing space alone also does not make stochastic continuous-time histories a finite-dimensional Riemannian manifold. The continuum functional determinant is therefore a further formal proposal, not an already defined extension of the finite construction.

## Recommended next bounded step

Retain the measured metric history object (Gamma, D, P). Treat smooth induced volume as one possible extent where its hypotheses hold, and retain finite-resolution coverings elsewhere.

Before another chemistry sweep, the smallest informative comparison is: one smooth contraction/expansion family with an analytic pullback volume, alongside the one-jump family with its exact metric covering geometry and native probability. Check coordinate/multiplicity handling and compare cumulative history with residual-at-cut geometry. These examples can establish what is genuinely being measured without changing the chemistry or choosing a desired winner.

The one-jump analysis also motivates geometric measure theory rather than forced smoothing for the existing CTMC. Neither route presently resolves the physically appropriate metric or establishes lushness. Both are principled, bounded ways to advance the requested possibility-volume calculation.
