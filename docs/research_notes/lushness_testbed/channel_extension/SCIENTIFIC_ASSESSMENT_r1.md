# Channel loss and shallow trapping: first scientific assessment

2026-10-13. Implementation was pushed as `924cae9`; this subsequent experiment
packet is local pending publication. Seven complete 36-table searches evaluated
252 policy/law combinations. Source/config snapshots and artifact hashes were
verified. All jobs stayed within 600 seconds, 1 GiB memory and 256 MiB output;
individual grids took 292–314 seconds and approximately 576–582 MiB peak memory.
The final focused engineering suite passed 49 tests.

## What the experiment establishes

The objective remains unfiltered sampled count-history breadth at cadence 0.25,
ranked by limiting rate and then rooted offset. Protection, usable stock and
recovery are readouts, never rewards. Controllers respond to local active
apparatus; the exhaustive optimum is certified only within the declared 36
shared response tables. Neither a valuer nor an open-ended ecology is represented.

Uniform absorbing exposure matches its analytic, policy-independent closure
curve to rounding error. Worn exposure makes preservation policy-dependent.
The worn absorbing breadth winner has 2.106% closure probability by time 64,
compared with 1.080% for passive idle. Its E8 seed preparation exceeds the matched
preparation reference by 5.476 bits at time 64 and by 18.094 bits in limiting
offset under that same policy and law. This is a measurable risk/breadth trade-off,
not a test of maximizing protection. Every absorbing policy eventually closes.

The retrospective planning-horizon check supports the predicted direction:
the worn absorbing one-step winner has 2.556% closure by the common deadline 64;
the four-, sixteen- and sixty-four-step winners have 2.237%, 1.753% and 1.493%.
This was not filed prospectively and should not be presented as preregistered
confirmation. The rate-first winner is a separate comparison, with 2.106% risk.

Recovery starts with all material trapped, empty fuel and service gates. Its
energy is four times the trap energy, so cross-depth recovery is a mechanism
contrast, not an E8-matched preparation comparison. First restoration is distinct
from having function at the deadline. A positive return path and zero permanent
closure do not imply useful or sustained operation.

## The tail is a physical-model question

Opus's claim that all absorbing policies share one limiting rate does not hold
in this adapter. Carriers, gates and controller hardware survive apparatus loss;
the policy still changes their transition law. Absorbing rates span 2.7366–4.8390
bits/time. His fixed service/mix25 table is reproduced: worn absorbing rate
3.56154 and distributed-root offset 1123.29145 bits. It is one table, not the grid.

Removing the tail, stopping the score at closure or adding a survival reward
would change the question. The principled next extension is paid controller
embodiment: make sensing, actuation and their continued operation depend on
physical resources and damage. Retain the surviving-hardware world as a control.
Then check whether the post-closure rate advantage survives that model change.
Correlated shocks remain the next disturbance module; they should be frozen
separately before inspecting new rankings. Criticality remains an untested
diagnostic hypothesis.

The experiment does not settle the ethical wager. It makes its relevant physical
trade-offs explicit without excluding randomness or defining the winner into
existence. The matched reference is a coarse count-state preparation ensemble,
not a molecular gas comparison. Four slots cannot establish open-ended compounding.
