and all-mobility steering had no alternative movement destination: their
alternatives were chemistry. They are chemistry-redirection experiments, not
mobility-only interventions. Of the 39 corridor-stable strength brackets,
18 concern these cases: access-exit (5), construction-exit (7), all-mobility (6).
Withdraw these 18 as mobility evidence. The remaining 21 are not thereby
certified as pure movement effects; entry steering can mix movement and chemistry.

and construction-exit rows, and S-positive disagreement rows (17 access-exit,

## Transfer, costs and controls

The original extract/cooperate channels were reversible receiver/donor pathways.
Their names did not impose net direction. The nominal cooperative channel moved
net resource toward D in the published regime: it is not evidence of donation.
Fixed-flow affinities supplied external work; they were not funded by D's bank.
The new pump has a separate physical fuel store charged from D's own bank.
Net transport and the net charging bill must both be measured before describing
a run as funded extraction or donation.

The old bill cap below one was an implementation restriction. Bills of one and
above are now permitted when the bank can physically accommodate the reaction.
A positive margin at 0.95 alone did not locate a break-even outside the interval.

Two old controls called sham have distinct physics: friction uses a driven
one-way bank debit; transfer uses reversible bank/bath exchange. New interfaces
accept descriptive aliases and reports retain the distinction. Historical D/S
banks were asymmetric; the new pump primary uses equal capacities and energy grids.

The sham beat moderate extraction on D breadth, not every extraction setting:
strong extraction's certified D gain exceeded the sham in the reported comparison.
No general preference for sham over extraction follows.
