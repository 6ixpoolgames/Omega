# Cost anchors for the first W1/W2 batch

This batch is a declared physical model, not a calibrated animal. Costs are
positive and explicit, and the same supply/bath prescription is used in paired
arms. Ratios are model assumptions rather than established physiological bounds.

Ramasamy, Allan and McCormick (2015), *Plasticity of Escape Responses*, provides
experimental evidence that threat cues alter escape responses. Its discussion
connects escape to energetic and foraging-opportunity costs. Killen et al.
(2015) experimentally links escape latency to metabolic recovery characteristics.
These support including cost, opportunity and recovery mechanisms; neither
determines a universal escape/foraging energy ratio for this toy.

- [Ramasamy et al., original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4526227/)
- [Killen et al., original article](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2015.00111/full)

Weststrate et al. (1990) measured increased diet-induced thermogenesis under a
stress-film condition but no significant change in resting metabolic rate.
Consequently a positive upkeep rate here is an explicit model regime, not a
claim that all aversive states necessarily raise basal energy expenditure by
at least 5 percent.

- [Weststrate et al., original experimental abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2364916/)

The registered first batch uses escape/forage bills of 1 or 2, and upkeep/basal
metabolism ratios of 0.05 or 0.25. These are the proposal's positive-cost model
box. Conclusions are conditional on that box and on the operational
tracking/disposition proxy; the box is not labelled empirically calibrated.
Zero-cost limits and a broader repertoire are excluded from this first batch,
not silently removed from the larger adversarial programme. No real-animal,
clinical or phenomenal-suffering conclusion follows from an arm's name.
