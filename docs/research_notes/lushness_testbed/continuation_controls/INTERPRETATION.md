# Corrected steering and matched exchange: interpretation

Both maps completed: sixteen corrected-steering cases and eight matched-transfer
cases, with source/configuration and numerical/resource checks passing. The
physical calibration chose flux targets without querying entropy. The studies
answer different questions and do not replace the previous signed results.

## Exchange contributes breadth without requiring net appropriation

At T256 the extraction pair has matched expected gross volumes of 4.429114 and
13.203192 events. Each level contains net movement toward S, weak net movement
toward D, stronger net movement toward D and a fitted zero-net point. The largest
actual gross-match error is 2.20e-8 events; zero-net residuals are around 1e-12.
These describe expected physical event counts, not cancelled labels or an
assumption that every reverse undoes a particular earlier transfer.

At the same gross volume, every tested directional point has lower whole-field
history breadth than its zero-net comparator at T256, at all three clocks:
eighteen strictly negative comparisons. At cadence 0.25 the low-volume
differences are -2.828 bits for the S-directed point, -1.385 for weak D-directed
flow and -17.307 for strong D-directed flow. At high volume they are -13.124,
-2.138 and -36.313 bits. This is a preference among tested points, not proof that
the continuous optimum is exactly zero net flow.

Strong D-directed flow also has certified lower S-history breadth than the
zero-net comparator at both volumes and all three clocks. D's own difference
against zero net remains unresolved. There is therefore no resolved D-positive,
S-negative conflict relative to zero net in this map.

Increasing exchange volume while holding the extraction pair at zero net gives
23.952 more whole-field bits at T256 and cadence 0.25. D and S also have
certified positive differences at all three clocks. Whole-field differences
are positive at every tested horizon and clock in that high-versus-low-volume
comparison. Thus net appropriation is not required for the transfer channel to
generate additional breadth. This supports the turnover explanation for the
earlier result, without uniquely assigning that earlier gain to turnover.

"Zero net" concerns the extraction pair. The retained basal cooperation pathway
still moves about 0.0007 resource units from S in the low-volume control and
0.00065 in the high-volume control. Those small residual flows are recorded;
the complete world is not claimed to have exactly zero net resource transfer.

## What matching establishes

The match applies at T256, from one common root, to extraction-pair gross counts.
It does not match total world jumps, native source throughput, heat or work.
For example, at low volume the S-directed point has 0.155 more total world
events than zero net yet loses 2.828 bits. Strong D-directed flow has 1.231 fewer
world events and loses 17.307 bits. Equal gross transfer count alone is not an
entropy decomposition or a guarantee of equal total activity.

Direction is changed through a declared external work affinity. Its work is
explicit in the first law and is disabled in the dark-equilibrium check. This
is a driven-law contrast, not a demonstration that D can buy directional bias
using its existing bank bill. At low gross volume, the strongly D-directed point
uses 6.182 units of net transfer-boundary work, whereas the zero-net control has
approximately zero net boundary work over the window. An equal-work comparison
and an embodied directional pump remain separate questions.

Horizon remains relevant. The weak D-directed low-volume point beats zero net
at T16 by a small, resolved amount at all clocks, then loses at later tested
horizons. The low-volume S-directed point has a positive limiting-rate difference
at the primary clock despite losing at the tested finite horizons. Rates are
diagnostics and do not replace these actual finite-history rankings.

## Corrected steering keeps its benefit in the tested range

Only outside-to-trap movement is targeted. Redirected attempts go only to other
movement; native chemistry is untouched at the same full state. Pocket-to-trap
return is not targeted. These corrections remove the two earlier interpretation
confounds without rewriting the original experiment.

All 144 intervention-versus-idle clock/horizon comparisons are whole-positive.
No tested yield bracket changes sign. At T256 and cadence 0.25, strong steering's
advantage falls from 33.461 bits at pocket multiplier 0 to 15.712 at multiplier
16. Strong blocking falls from 32.647 to 14.928. Greater pocket productivity
therefore reduces the benefit of avoiding the trap but does not reverse it here.

Arrival at the pocket falls under intervention. At multiplier 16, strong steering
also reduces S's net capture by 0.179 resource units through T256, while its
whole-field breadth rises. S's personal breadth is unresolved in that high-yield
regime; whole-field benefit must not be substituted for a proven personal gain.

The multiplier speeds both forward and reverse capture; it is not sixteen times
the energy delivered per source packet. The source remains finite in throughput,
and discovery does not unlock a persistent new construction/renewal pathway.
This test therefore does not establish a boundary for restricting a recursively
generative opportunity. The declared range is exhausted; no further multiplier
was chosen to force a crossing.

## Scope

These maps use an explicit half-unit D-bank grid, rather than the previous
quarter/tenth grids. They are new adapter comparisons, not numerical replays of
those earlier worlds. Active steering retains its coarse driven boundary;
the transfer reactions have native reverses and satisfy the dark checks.
No irreversible loss, population, suffering, consent or resistance module was
added. All physical randomness counts. Small resolved differences stand, and
unresolved marginal-history bounds remain unresolved.

Compact full maps, physical matching records, figures, source archives and
receipts are retained. No dense kernels or inferred ethical rewards are added.
