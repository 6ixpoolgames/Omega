# What the robust-compounding run establishes

**Updated interpretation — 15 October 2026.** Replaces the earlier combined-
checklist failure framing. Physical laws, numerical results and original
checklist values are retained; the scientific conclusion follows the mechanism.

The run demonstrates three successive paid enabling links with retained
expansion. In four distinct regimes, compounding coexists with substantial
functional persistence and greater finite-history lushness than both the
unbound preparation and the main mechanism ablations. This establishes the
intended physical conjunction within this finite count chemistry.

The experiment maps how that mechanism depends on source strength, feedback
and damage. Our earlier interpretation confused a provisional checklist with
the principle: an arbitrary 80% external-funding certificate at T256 made every
combined flag zero, despite the demonstrated mechanism. That was a poor
operational test, not a failure of compounding. We now lead with the physical
evidence and report funding, persistence and recovery as measured profiles.

## Three links and their physical consequence

All 12 shape settings demonstrate the conservative depth-three-plus-retained-
expansion event, including square-root feedback. Across the shape grid, cutting
each construction link reduces unlabelled retained expansion in at least one
setting. These physical ablations connect the observed growth to the enabling
cycle, beyond the tautology that deleting a named link deletes its certificate.

In the linear, full-assistance, source2.4 example:

The breadth signs above hold at all three evaluated cadences. The tracker
is a query over native reactions, adds no physical entropy and can miss many
true chains. These probabilities are conservative certificates, not full
genealogy or a claim that most paths complete the monitored lineage.

## Stress breaks retention

At linear/full assistance and source 0.6, increasing ordinary damage gives:

| Damage rate | Depth AND retained expansion at 256 | Uptime 64–256 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.005 | 10.015% | 50.36% |
| 0.02 | 0.884% | 11.28% |
| 0.08 | 0.000784% | 0.100% |

Three-link events still occur under stronger damage: for example, completion
alone at damage 0.02 is 9.84%. Most of that accomplishment is no longer retained
at 256. This separates compounding while operational from durable retained gains.

The 24 elevated-stress settings show reduced retention and persistence, while
the full mechanism improves unconditional restoration relative to the C-to-A
cut in the tested comparisons. In the source 0.6 linear pulse arm, 22.45% of the
initial ensemble is functional before the pulse; conditional endpoint recovery
64 later is 41.70%, and the joint pre-function/restoration probability is 9.36%.
The full ensemble remains in the denominator where appropriate.

Four distinct shape regimes sustain expected functional uptime above one half
over 64–256, alongside compounding and positive lushness consequences. Four R0
settings reuse shape cases, so repeated rows are not independent replications.
The continuous damage and recovery profiles describe the operating range more
informatively than the original guessed pass cutoffs.

## Funding develops over the continuation

Construction and maintenance pay their physical energy bills. The proposed
80% external-spending lower bound at T256 was an additional bookkeeping guess,
not a physical requirement for generativity. The conservative bound
deducts the whole possible contribution of the initial packet, thermal packet
return and reverse capture, as well as final stored charge.

For the source 2.4 linear example, the gross-capture spending lower bound is
87.30%, but deducting the non-external packet budget reduces the external bound
to 75.39%. That budget is 3.44 energy units, including the initial 3-unit packet.
The later profiles give external lower bounds of 87.70% at 1024
and 92.67% at 4096. Initial-resource amortization therefore matters.

The initial packet is a legitimate resource in the present frame. Its use
does not disqualify compounding. The longer profiles show external funding
taking over as initial resources are amortized. An 80% guess at one early
horizon should never have been allowed to negate that physical result.
A tighter provenance query could refine the funding account; the current
bound already establishes substantial ongoing external support.

## The references answer different questions

At T256 the full mechanism beats the unbound preparation and both main mechanism
controls across all cadences in all 40 settings. It beats the full thermal
reference in none. The thermal reference has mean initial apparatus 4.2577,
with a complete loop already present with probability 47.17%; the seed has one
apparatus and the unbound root has none. All three start with energy 7.

The thermal reference starts with more machinery and remains the lusher
preparation in these comparisons. It is an
equienergy count-state mixture that naturally contains substantial machinery,
not a disordered spatial gas. No translational motion exists in this adapter.
Neither the positive unbound result nor this thermal ordering resolves the earlier
spatial lattice reversal. Same-law roots share a limiting rate; their advantages
are finite profiles and offsets. Mechanism ablations can change the limiting
rate, but also alter accepted work. This batch matches offered source and bath,
not accepted work or event activity.

## Persistence relationship and adverse readouts

The measured persistence association is positive at every anchor: Spearman correlations
between functional uptime and the declared breadth advantages range 0.821–1.0.
This is descriptive evidence for the proposed relationship. It is not proof of
a general persistence/repayment identity. The source-window experiment was not
run; negative formal asymptotic crossover estimates do not supply a positive
physical repayment deadline.

The ordinary-damage comparisons show no breadth increase accompanied by reduced
uptime under the original adjacency check. A broader irreversibility audit adds
an adverse observation:
four of eight contrasts gain T256 breadth across all cadences while losing
functional uptime. For example, at alpha0.5/source0.15, changing repairable
damage to 5% permanent loss adds 1.168 bits at 256 while reducing uptime. Its sign
is still positive at 1024 and negative at 4096 (−2.460 bits at cadence 0.25).
This is a cross-law finite-horizon finding. It exposes a real distinction
between short-run branching and functional preservation; it does not model an
agent choosing destruction. Its reversal over the longer horizon matters to
the interpretation regardless of when this particular comparison was suggested.

Only 18 of 40 settings satisfy the 0.001-bit offset-tail diagnostic by 4096; the
largest remaining tail is about 3527 bits in the slow material-loss cases.
Finite scores are evaluated directly through the exact sampled-history identity,
so their meaning does not depend on an offset already having settled. The
limiting rate is not used to override these finite comparisons.

## Engineering and next boundary

All 144 configured cases completed in 32.9 minutes of summed capped worker time,
maximum 352.9 MiB. All frozen source bundles and copied outputs were verified.
Maximum energy and bank residuals were 1.23e-11 and 1.79e-12; maximum monitor
projection error 4.44e-16. Coordinate contributions sum to physical entropy
within 5.3e-10. Thirty-six focused tests pass. All physical randomness remains
in the objective; there are no protection rewards or Rule A filters.

The next substantive boundary is costed persistence/reconstruction under stress,
with its dependence on redundancy and opportunity windows made explicit. A
tighter funding query and spatial replication remain distinct tasks. This
batch establishes a finite-depth physical mechanism and its bounded regime;
it does not establish robust open-ended compounding, network superiority,
valuers or an ethical verdict.
