# From Historical Identity to Valuerhood

Where Omega Meets FEP

## Purpose

This note defines the handoff between the primitive floor clarified in `relation_as_historical_binding.md` and Omega proper.

The primitive revision says:

```text
distinction:
  differences exist

asymmetry:
  different paths produce different consequences

relation:
  causal continuity links differences across transformation
```

This gives the minimum vocabulary for historical identity. But Omega should not try to derive all of agency from this floor. That descent quickly enters territory already developed by the Free Energy Principle, active inference, autopoiesis, and related self-maintenance frameworks.

The point of this note is to mark the boundary:

> Omega inherits the FEP floor. Omega begins above it.

Omega is not primarily a theory of how bounded agency first emerges. Omega is a theory of what happens once recoverable self-maintaining histories exist: which value-bearing trajectories remain viable, compatible, recoverable, and rich under physical, epistemic, resource, scale, and inter-history constraints.

## The primitive floor

The primitive layer establishes only the conditions under which identity can be discussed at all.

A sequence of states is not yet an identity:

```text
X0 -> X1 -> X2 -> ... -> Xt
```

A process becomes identity-like when causal continuity across transformation coheres into a continuable pattern. A vortex is the guiding example: its constituents churn, but the causal organization remains continuous enough for the vortex to be the same process.

This is historical identity:

> a distinction-bearing process whose causal organization remains coherently continuous through transformation.

But historical identity alone is not enough. Stasis, clocks, cycles, attractors, and local phase memories can all mimic persistence. The floor relevant to agency is not mere persistence but recoverable historical identity:

> identity-through-transformation that remains continuable under perturbation.

This is where the primitive layer meets the FEP floor.

## What Omega inherits from FEP

FEP and related self-maintenance theories answer the lower question:

> How can a bounded system maintain its identity under perturbation?

Omega should inherit, rather than replace, this answer.

The inherited floor is:

```text
boundedness:
  some distinction between process and environment is maintained

self-maintenance:
  the system acts or updates in ways that preserve its continued existence

perturbation resistance:
  identity is not immediately erased by noise, disturbance, or uncertainty

model / state coupling:
  internal states remain coupled enough to external conditions to support continued viability

continuability:
  the system remains able to proceed as itself through time
```

Omega does not need to prove from first principles that such systems can exist. Nor does it need to outcompete FEP as a theory of minimal agency.

Instead, Omega can take the following as its floor:

> A valuer candidate is a bounded historical identity that maintains recoverable continuability under perturbation.

## What Omega does not try to prove

This boundary matters because the project can otherwise drift downward indefinitely.

Omega does not need to prove:

```text
how agency arises from arbitrary matter
how boundaries first form in full generality
how all self-maintaining systems implement active inference
how consciousness emerges
how preferences arise psychologically
how every minimal persistent process becomes a valuer
```

Those are adjacent questions. Some are already central to FEP, autopoiesis, origins-of-life research, cybernetics, and cognitive science.

Omega's contribution begins after the minimal self-maintaining floor:

> Given systems that can preserve themselves as bounded histories, what trajectory structures remain viable and value-bearing over long horizons?

## Minimal valuerhood

The older Omega drafts often assume valuers, agents, or observers. This note supplies a minimal bridge.

A valuer is not defined by consciousness, explicit preference, reward maximization, or human-like cognition.

Proposed minimal definition:

> A valuer is a bounded historical identity for which different continuations asymmetrically affect recoverable continuability.

This has five components:

```text
1. boundary:
   there is enough distinction between the process and its environment for identity to be meaningful

2. causal continuity:
   identity is organized through transformation rather than fixed constituents

3. recoverability:
   perturbations do not immediately destroy continuability

4. self-maintaining interaction:
   interaction with the environment contributes to continued identity

5. path consequence:
   different continuations change the future viability of the process
```

This definition is intentionally minimal. It says only that the process has a stake-like structure: its continuability can be preserved, degraded, restored, or lost.

This is the point where persistence becomes proto-value.

Not because the system has moral status in the human sense. Not because it has preferences in the psychological sense. But because there is now a coherent identity for which continuation and collapse differ.

## Recoverability as the real floor

Mere persistence is too weak.

A rock persists. A clock persists. A stable attractor persists. A crystal persists. But these do not necessarily instantiate the kind of valuerhood Omega needs.

The relevant floor is recoverable persistence:

```text
not just:
  the state remains

but:
  the identity remains continuable under perturbation
```

Recoverability matters because it distinguishes passive endurance from active or proto-active self-maintenance.

A valuer-like process must be able to absorb disturbance, preserve boundary conditions, or find continuation paths after disruption. This does not require cognition. But it does require that the process's identity is not merely a static configuration or simple cycle.

This is why the primitive fakeout tests matter:

```text
stasis:
  persists but does not transform

clock:
  repeats but compresses to phase

local phase memory:
  cycles but does not require causal-historical depth

self-only recurrence:
  persists without relational organization

lock-in:
  survives by collapsing future possibility

noise:
  changes without identity
```

Recoverable historical identity is the first non-cheap floor.

## Omega proper

Omega begins when valuers are treated as histories embedded in constraint.

The central Omega question is not:

> How does a bounded system maintain itself at all?

The central Omega question is:

> Among bounded self-maintaining valuers, which trajectories remain viable, recoverable, compatible, and rich under uncertainty?

Omega proper concerns:

```text
recoverable reachability:
  continuable futures remain accessible without irreversible collapse

distinguishable viable futures:
  future richness is structured, not merely numerous

bounded cost:
  persistence remains physically and computationally feasible

epistemic grounding:
  models remain coupled to reality enough to guide continuation

scale consistency:
  abstraction preserves real viability and does not invent fake viability

inter-history compatibility:
  histories interact without destroying each other's recoverable continuability

mutual corridors:
  interacting trajectories preserve or expand compatible future passage

pseudo-Omega attractors:
  locally persistent structures that preserve themselves by degrading broader value-bearing reachability

redemption / re-entry:
  damaged histories remain reconnectable to Omega-compatible futures before irreversible horizon crossing

lushness:
  the richness and diversity of compatible value-bearing trajectory space
```

This is the original center of the project.

Viability is the gate. The prize is not mere survival, but the maximal physically admissible richness of value-bearing trajectory space.

## Relation to the older drafts

The earlier Omega drafts were right to begin above the primitive floor.

They focused on:

```text
viable trajectories
recoverability
reachability
cost
belief / epistemic viability
scale consistency
distinguishable viable futures
multi-agent compatibility
pseudo-Omega failure modes
```

That emphasis remains correct.

The primitive revision only clarifies what was implicit: before any of these constraints can be value-relevant, there must be a bounded historical identity whose continuation can matter.

The stack is therefore:

```text
primitive substrate:
  distinction, path consequence, causal continuity

recoverable historical identity:
  identity-through-transformation under perturbation

FEP / self-maintenance floor:
  bounded systems maintain continuability

valuerhood:
  bounded historical identities whose continuations can preserve or degrade recoverable continuability

Omega:
  viable, compatible, richly structured value-bearing trajectory space
```

## Minimal worlds after the handoff

Minimal cellular automata and rule-space probes should be interpreted narrowly.

They are useful for floor calibration:

```text
What is persistence?
What is a fakeout?
What does causal continuity through turnover look like?
How do clocks, local phase cycles, stasis, and self-only recurrence fool detectors?
What would recoverable historical identity require?
```

They are not Omega validation.

The q3/r1 branch showed this clearly. It found rare held-out positives and helped clarify fakeout controls, but the fertile-band prediction failed and the substrate remains far below valuerhood.

So minimal worlds should not carry the burden of proving Omega. They are microscopes for the primitive floor.

Above the FEP floor, empirical validation should move to systems that already instantiate minimal self-maintenance:

```text
homeostatic agents
active-inference agents
autopoietic toy systems
reachability / empowerment agents
evolutionary artificial-life systems
multi-agent viability worlds
```

These are the right arenas for Omega's actual questions.

## Experimental implication

The next empirical validation program should not ask:

> Can a cellular automaton motif become Omega-like?

It should ask:

> Given minimal valuers, do Omega constraints predict which trajectories persist, collapse, recover, become trapped, or remain mutually compatible?

Baselines should include:

```text
raw survival
raw reachability
raw empowerment
reward maximization
short-horizon viability
local persistence
```

Omega predictors should include:

```text
recoverable reachability
distinguishable viable futures
bounded cost
epistemic grounding
scale consistency
inter-history compatibility
```

The validation target becomes:

> Omega constraints predict long-horizon viability and failure modes of valuers better than simpler local or reward-like baselines.

## Final thesis

Omega does not replace FEP.

FEP describes the self-maintaining floor: bounded identities that remain continuable under perturbation.

Omega begins above that floor. It studies the structure of value-bearing histories: which valuers, interactions, and trajectory ensembles remain viable, recoverable, compatible, and rich under uncertainty.

In compressed form:

```text
primitive floor:
  distinction + path consequence + causal continuity

agency floor:
  recoverable self-maintaining identity

Omega proper:
  viable value-bearing trajectory structure across time, scale, and interaction
```

This boundary keeps the theory from drifting downward into a full theory of agency emergence, while preserving the original ambition: identifying the conditions under which value-bearing possibility can continue and flourish.
