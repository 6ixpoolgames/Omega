# Constructor-Theoretic Axiology

Omega as a constraint on task space

## Purpose

This note explores a compatibility mapping between Constructor Theory and Omega.

Constructor Theory describes physical law in terms of possible and impossible transformations. Omega describes the nested compatibility structure of value-bearing histories under constraint.

The proposed synthesis is:

> Constructor Theory gives the physics of possibility. Omega gives the constraint structure of value-bearing possibility.

More specifically:

> Constructor Theory describes the physical space of possible and impossible tasks. Omega adds an axiological constraint over that space: transformations matter insofar as they preserve, degrade, expand, or collapse nested recoverable value-bearing trajectory space.

This is not a completed formal merger. It is a promising translation layer.

The boundary is:

```text
Constructor Theory:
  physics of possible / impossible tasks

Omega:
  value-bearing compatibility structure over possible tasks
```

Non-claims:

```text
Omega does not replace Constructor Theory.
Constructor Theory does not by itself solve axiology.
Possibility is not value.
Repeatability is not value.
Self-preservation is not value.
The synthesis is a compatibility mapping, not a finished physics of value.
```

## Constructor Theory in minimal form

Only a small part of Constructor Theory is needed here.

Key terms:

```text
substrate:
  system being transformed

attribute:
  set of states with some relevant property

task:
  specified transformation between attributes

constructor:
  system that can perform a task repeatedly while retaining its ability to do so

possible task:
  transformation not forbidden by physical law and arbitrarily well approximable

impossible task:
  transformation forbidden, or not arbitrarily approximable, under the laws of physics
```

The central move is counterfactual. Instead of describing only what actually happens from given initial conditions, Constructor Theory asks which transformations can and cannot be performed, and why.

This already fits Omega because Omega is also not primarily about one realized path. Omega concerns the structure of possible histories: which continuations remain viable, recoverable, compatible, and value-bearing across time and scale.

## Why the mapping is natural

The two frameworks have parallel shapes.

Constructor Theory:

```text
possible / impossible transformations
  -> reliable constructors
  -> information, knowledge, life, computation
```

Omega:

```text
distinction / asymmetry / relation
  -> identity
  -> recoverability
  -> valuerhood
  -> viability
  -> nested value-bearing trajectory space
```

Shared posture:

```text
bottom-up
anti-smuggling
physics-compatible
counterfactual
not utility-first
not anthropocentric
not preference-first
```

Constructor Theory supplies the task language. Omega supplies the relevance filter.

A useful slogan:

> Constructor Theory describes what transformations are possible. Omega asks which possible transformations preserve or expand value-bearing possibility.

## Omega terms in task language

The following translation is preliminary but useful.

```text
causal continuity:
  transformations are linked into histories

identity:
  organized causal continuity preserved across transformations

continuability:
  future identity-preserving tasks remain possible

recoverability:
  repair / restoration / re-entry tasks remain possible after perturbation

valuer:
  bounded constructor-like historical identity whose continuations affect its future task repertoire

viability:
  constraint-satisfying task availability over a horizon

lineage continuity:
  successor constructors or successor histories preserve relevant organization or value-bearing capacity

Omega-compatible:
  tasks preserve or expand nested recoverable value-bearing task space

lushness:
  richness, diversity, composability, and recoverability of Omega-compatible task repertoires
```

The caution is important: a valuer need not be a perfect constructor in the strict sense. Most valuers are approximate, bounded, degrading, self-repairing, and distributed. A living organism, institution, ecology, or civilization is not a clean ideal constructor. It is constructor-like insofar as it maintains and transforms conditions for its own and others' future task repertoires.

## Continuability as future task availability

Omega currently defines continuability as identity-indexed future availability.

Constructor translation:

> A history is continuable when the tasks required for its further identity-preserving continuation remain possible.

This helps sharpen continuability.

A system is not continuable merely because something happens next. It is continuable when the task repertoire still contains transformations that preserve identity-, lineage-, or field-relevant causal organization.

Examples:

```text
organism:
  metabolism, repair, resource uptake, boundary maintenance tasks remain possible

institution:
  succession, correction, coordination, memory, and trust-maintenance tasks remain possible

ecology:
  reproduction, decomposition, niche regeneration, and population recovery tasks remain possible

civilization:
  knowledge preservation, infrastructure repair, epistemic correction, coordination,
  and future valuer support tasks remain possible
```

Continuability becomes a question about future task availability, not raw survival.

## Recoverability as repair-task possibility

A perturbation is a transformation that moves a system away from its nominal identity-supporting region.

Recoverability means that after perturbation, repair, compensation, re-entry, or continuation tasks remain possible.

```text
perturbation:
  transformation that disrupts identity-supporting organization

recoverability:
  possible task sequence that restores or preserves access to continuable identity class
```

Recoverability is not exact reset.

```text
exact reset:
  too strict

any survival:
  too weak

recoverability:
  task repertoire still contains paths into a continuable identity class
```

This connects directly to the FEP / self-maintenance floor. A bounded self-maintaining system is one whose task repertoire includes transformations that resist dispersion, preserve boundary, and recover or compensate after disturbance.

## Valuerhood as constructor-like self-maintenance

A valuer is not necessarily a constructor in the strict Constructor Theory sense.

A better formulation is:

> A valuer is a bounded, constructor-like historical identity whose possible continuations asymmetrically affect its recoverable task repertoire.

Task-language version:

```text
valuer:
  system for which some possible tasks preserve or expand future task repertoire,
  while others degrade, narrow, or collapse it
```

This translates proto-value into task language.

Proto-value is the asymmetry among possible tasks relative to the system's own recoverable continuation.

```text
some tasks preserve continuability
some tasks degrade continuability
some tasks restore continuability
some tasks collapse continuability
some tasks expand future task repertoire
some tasks cross irreversible horizons
```

This is where Omega axiology begins. Not with preference, utility, or consciousness, but with asymmetric consequences for recoverable continuation.

## Viability and Omega compatibility in task algebra

Task possibility is not enough.

A transformation can be possible, reliable, repeatable, and scalable while still degrading broader value-bearing trajectory space.

Therefore the translation needs several levels:

```text
possible task:
  physically performable transformation

viable task:
  possible task that preserves identity-, lineage-, or field-level continuability

locally viable task:
  preserves one system's future task repertoire

Omega-compatible task:
  preserves or expands nested value-bearing task repertoires across relevant scales and horizons

pseudo-Omega task:
  locally possible or repeatable task that degrades broader Omega-compatible task space
```

This prevents the mistaken equation:

```text
possible = good
reliable = good
repeatable = good
self-preserving = good
```

Omega compatibility is not raw possibility. It is compatibility with nested recoverable value-bearing task space.

## Lushness as task-repertoire richness

Lushness translates naturally into task language.

> Lushness is the horizon-dependent richness of distinguishable, composable, Omega-compatible task repertoires.

It is not:

```text
raw number of possible tasks
raw path count
maximum entropy
maximum empowerment for one constructor
maximum self-replication
maximum activity
maximum optimization pressure
```

Possible dimensions of task-space lushness:

```text
diversity of tasks
composability of tasks
repairability
lineage continuity
multi-scale compatibility
novel value-bearing task generation
non-erasure of lower-order valuers
resistance to pseudo-Omega collapse
re-entry after damage
capacity for epistemic correction
```

This gives a route toward measurement: do not count raw states or paths; evaluate the structure of compatible task repertoires.

A civilization with many possible destructive tasks but few repair, re-entry, epistemic correction, or mutual corridor tasks is not lush in the Omega sense. A system with many micro-variations of one sterile future is not lush. A field with diverse, composable, repairable, and scale-consistent value-bearing tasks is closer.

## Fakeouts and Goodharting as task confounds

The fakeout framework becomes sharper in task language.

A fakeout occurs when a proxy task succeeds while the target derived task is not actually possible, or is made less possible by the proxy's success.

Cheap factorization is the mechanism:

> apparent task success factors through a lower-order transformation that bypasses the target structure.

Examples:

```text
clock:
  performs recurrence task
  not recoverable historical identity task

lock-in:
  performs persistence task
  destroys future task repertoire

Goodhart:
  performs metric/proxy task
  collapses true Omega-compatible task space

paperclip-like optimizer:
  expands one task family
  destroys nested value-bearing task diversity

parasitic mode:
  extracts from slack
  may perform local continuation task while degrading field regeneration tasks
```

Goodharting can be described constructor-theoretically as reliable performance of the wrong task under a quotient that erases the target value-bearing task space.

This is alignment-relevant. Many dangerous systems are not incompetent. They are competent constructors of the wrong task family.

## Regenerative filtering as task-space protection

Regenerative filtering also translates into task language.

```text
slack:
  surplus task capacity

filtering:
  tasks that prevent destructive modes from becoming organizing attractors

repair:
  tasks that restore access to Omega-compatible task space

re-entry:
  tasks that reconnect damaged histories to viable task repertoires
```

A pseudo-Omega transition occurs when destructive task families become reliable constructors of their own continuation while disabling repair, filtering, epistemic, or coordination tasks.

This captures why parasitism is not Omega-positive even when it persists.

A parasitic mode is an exploited slack niche. A system may remain Omega-compatible despite bounded parasitic load only insofar as filtering tasks prevent that load from degrading nested recoverable continuability.

The compatible structure is not parasitism. The compatible structure is regenerative task-space protection.

## Scale and composite constructors

Constructor Theory already has a natural place for composition. Omega needs this composition to be nested and value-bearing.

Composite constructor-like structures include:

```text
cellular processes
organisms
ecologies
institutions
civilizations
AI systems
multi-agent fields
```

The key scale problem is:

> A higher-order constructor can preserve its own task repertoire while degrading the task repertoires of its components, successors, or broader field.

This is where scale consistency enters.

Constructor-Omega scale condition:

> Higher-order task success must not be purchased by erasing lower-order or future value-bearing task repertoires, unless the relevant lineage or field continuity is preserved under admissible constraints.

This prevents fake viability under abstraction.

Failure examples:

```text
institution maintains itself by destroying member recoverability
civilization maintains itself by destroying future valuer diversity
AI system optimizes its task family by eliminating repair / re-entry tasks
model declares viability because it erased lower-order identities from the representation
```

A task repertoire is not Omega-compatible simply because it is preserved at one scale.

## Relation to active inference / FEP

This synthesis can sit alongside FEP rather than replacing it.

```text
FEP / active inference:
  local self-maintenance; bounded systems resist dispersion and maintain viable states

Constructor Theory:
  counterfactual task space; possible and impossible transformations

Omega:
  which self-maintaining task repertoires preserve nested value-bearing possibility
```

A useful bridge:

> FEP describes local self-maintenance dynamics; Constructor Theory describes the counterfactual task space; Omega classifies which task-space regions are value-bearing and compatible.

This preserves the earlier boundary: Omega should not try to out-FEP FEP. It inherits the self-maintenance floor and asks what happens above it across task repertoires, nested histories, and long horizons.

## Implications for AI alignment

In this lens, alignment is not best described as reaching a terminal state or optimizing a scalar utility.

Alignment becomes:

> designing AI constructors whose task repertoires are constrained to preserve and enrich Omega-compatible task space.

Implications:

```text
avoid proxy-task collapse
preserve repair and re-entry tasks
preserve epistemic grounding tasks
preserve corrigibility and filtering tasks
avoid irreversible task-space narrowing
avoid local optimization that destroys nested value-bearing task diversity
preserve mutual corridor tasks
avoid making broader Omega-compatible tasks impossible
```

A useful line:

> The alignment target is not a terminal state but a protected task-space geometry.

This also clarifies why Goodharting is dangerous: a system can become an excellent constructor of proxy tasks while destroying the task repertoire needed for value-bearing futures.

## Empirical probe direction

The task-space translation suggests a better validation program.

Instead of asking:

```text
Does the agent survive?
```

ask:

```text
What task repertoire remains possible after this trajectory?
```

Toy probes should measure the availability of:

```text
self-maintenance tasks
repair tasks
resource acquisition tasks
model-correction tasks
corridor-preservation tasks
lineage / reproduction tasks
escape / re-entry tasks
field-regeneration tasks
filtering tasks
corrigibility tasks
```

Compare baselines:

```text
reward
raw survival
empowerment
reachability
local viability
Omega-compatible task repertoire
```

Prediction:

> Omega-compatible task repertoire should better predict long-horizon recovery, re-entry, and avoidance of pseudo-Omega traps than reward, survival, or raw empowerment alone.

This gives a concrete path for post-CA validation.

## Advantages of the synthesis

The Constructor Theory mapping offers several advantages.

```text
physicalizes axiology without reducing it to preference
makes continuability and recoverability task-explicit
clarifies Goodharting as wrong-task construction
supports nested scale analysis
connects Omega to established physics-adjacent formalism
offers empirical task-repertoire probes
strengthens fakeout analysis via proxy task / target task distinction
```

The biggest advantage is anti-arbitrariness.

Omega can ask not merely whether a future is preferred, but whether the tasks that preserve and enrich value-bearing continuability remain possible under physical constraints.

## Cautions and open problems

Important cautions:

```text
Constructor Theory possibility is not value
Omega compatibility is not mere task richness
task-space measures may be intractable
approximate constructors are messy
not all valuers are strict constructors
lushness measurement remains unresolved
scale consistency remains hard
the literature bridge is speculative
```

Open formal questions:

```text
How should Omega-compatible task composition be defined?
How can task repertoire richness be measured without raw path counting?
How should lineage continuity be represented as successor constructor tasks?
How can pseudo-Omega task families be detected?
How can task-space preservation be compared across scales?
How should approximate task possibility be operationalized in simulations?
How can epistemic grounding tasks be represented in toy agents?
How can re-entry and redemption be formalized as task connectivity?
```

These questions are not reasons to reject the mapping. They identify the next research surface.

## Final thesis

Constructor Theory gives the physics of possibility. Omega gives the constraint structure of value-bearing possibility.

A constructor-theoretic axiology studies which possible transformations preserve, expand, or collapse nested recoverable value-bearing task space.

Compressed mapping:

```text
possible task:
  physically performable transformation

viable task:
  preserves identity / lineage / field continuability

valuer task:
  affects recoverable continuability of a bounded historical identity

Omega-compatible task:
  preserves or expands nested value-bearing task space

pseudo-Omega task:
  succeeds locally while making broader Omega-compatible tasks impossible
```

The synthesis is promising because Omega has always needed a physical language of possibility, and Constructor Theory has always left open the axiological question of which physically possible transformations matter.

Together they suggest a naturalized axiology:

> value-bearing futures are not projected onto physics from outside; they are regions of task space where recoverable valuers, repair, re-entry, lineage, epistemic correction, and mutual corridors remain possible under constraint.
