# Omega as Viable Value-Bearing Trajectory Space

A theory-center note

## Purpose

This note recenters Omega after the primitive/FEP/valuerhood bridge.

The preceding notes clarified the lower stack:

```text
primitive floor:
  distinction + path consequence + causal continuity

agency floor:
  recoverable self-maintaining identity

valuerhood:
  bounded historical identity whose continuations asymmetrically affect recoverable continuability
```

This note returns to Omega proper.

Omega does not begin at bare persistence, cellular automata motifs, or minimal agency. Omega begins once valuers exist and enter trajectory space together. It concerns the nested, scale-spanning structure of value-bearing histories that remain recoverably continuable under physical, epistemic, resource, scale, and inter-history constraints.

The purpose of this note is to capture the full object, not only a local or zoomed-in piece of it.

## Core thesis

Omega is the asymptotic compatibility structure of value-bearing trajectory space.

More explicitly:

> Omega is the nested, scale-spanning structure of value-bearing histories and inter-histories that remain recoverably continuable under physical, epistemic, resource, scale, and inter-history constraints.

This is not local survival. It is not a single optimal future. It is not a utility function. It is not simply the set of things that persist.

Omega concerns the whole trajectory geometry of value-bearing possibility:

```text
valuers emerge
histories persist or collapse
histories interact
interactions become higher-order histories
corridors open or close
traps and pseudo-Omega structures form
scale transitions preserve or destroy viability
some futures remain recoverably continuable
others cross horizons of irreversible loss
```

A compact slogan:

> Viability is the gate; nested, compatible, value-bearing trajectory richness is the prize.

## Omega as a nested trajectory field

Omega is not a flat collection of agents or trajectories. It is nested.

Histories compose into higher-order histories. A valuer's path is a history. Interactions among valuers form histories. Institutions, ecologies, cultures, civilizations, and technological systems are higher-order histories built from interacting lower-order histories. These higher-order histories then reshape the future reachability of the histories composing them.

The recursive structure is:

```text
state/event
  -> causal continuity
  -> history
  -> interacting histories
  -> higher-order history
  -> trajectory field
  -> asymptotic compatibility structure
```

At each scale, continuability can be preserved or destroyed.

A rough scale map:

```text
primitive scale:
  causal continuity links differences across transformation

identity scale:
  organized causal continuity remains coherent through change

organismic / agentic scale:
  valuers maintain recoverable continuability

inter-agent scale:
  histories interact, forming corridors, traps, conflicts, cooperation, repair paths

ecological / institutional scale:
  stable higher-order histories shape lower-order reachability

civilizational scale:
  long-horizon trajectory fields coordinate infrastructures, knowledge, norms, and agents

asymptotic scale:
  Omega-compatible futures persist under broad physical, epistemic, and inter-history constraints
```

The same object is visible at every scale, but not as identical repetition. Each scale introduces new constraints and new failure modes. A person can remain locally viable while an institution collapses. An institution can persist by degrading its members. A civilization can survive by narrowing future valuer diversity. A technological attractor can stabilize itself while consuming the broader field of recoverable possibility.

Omega is the structure that remains when viability must be compatible across these nested scales.

## Alpha-Omega: emergence to asymptotic persistence

The original intuitive object is not merely Omega as an endpoint. It is Alpha-Omega: the full arc of value-bearing trajectory space from emergence to asymptotic persistence.

```text
Alpha:
  emergence of distinction-preserving, recoverably continuable valuers

Omega:
  asymptotic compatibility structure of value-bearing trajectories

Alpha-Omega:
  the nested trajectory geometry from emergence to long-horizon compatible persistence
```

Alpha is already shown in the existence of valuers: organisms, agents, observers, societies, and other processes that maintain recoverable continuability. Omega is not yet shown. Omega is the limiting structure of histories that remain compatible with value-bearing continuation under increasing horizon, interaction, uncertainty, cost, and scale.

Thus Omega is not a destination in ordinary state space. It is a constraint-shaped trajectory field.

## Starting object: valuers, not states

Omega does not operate over raw states.

A state can be useful for modeling, but it is not the fundamental object of Omega. A state has no value-bearing meaning unless it belongs to a history that can continue, recover, interact, and matter to some valuer or valuer-like process.

The relevant starting objects are:

```text
bounded historical identities
recoverable continuability
valuer trajectory classes
inter-history structures
```

From the valuerhood bridge:

> A valuer is a bounded historical identity for which different continuations asymmetrically affect recoverable continuability.

Omega asks what happens when such histories are embedded in a shared world with finite energy, uncertainty, irreversible loss, imperfect models, scale transformations, and other valuers.

The contrast is:

```text
state-based view:
  asks which state is best

trajectory-space view:
  asks which histories remain continuable, compatible, and value-bearing
```

Omega is trajectory-space first.

## Viability is the gate, not the prize

Omega is not whatever survives.

Survival is necessary because value cannot be instantiated where no valuer or value-bearing distinction can continue. But survival alone is insufficient.

Viability should be indexed to identity, lineage, or field. It is not only literal individual persistence.

```text
direct viability:
  an identity itself remains continuable

recoverable viability:
  perturbations do not erase access to the identity's continuable class

lineage viability:
  successor histories preserve enough causal organization or value-bearing capacity

field viability:
  the broader ecology or trajectory field remains capable of generating, sustaining,
  repairing, and composing value-bearing histories
```

There are many ways to persist badly:

```text
bare survival:
  something continues, but with little value-bearing richness

local viability:
  a trajectory continues on its own terms while losing broader compatibility

trap viability:
  a system remains stable but loses recoverable futures

parasitic persistence:
  one structure preserves itself by exploiting an exposed slack niche

lock-in viability:
  continuation is maintained by collapsing diversity and future option space
```

The distinction is:

```text
viability:
  the gate through which value-bearing histories must pass

lushness:
  the horizon-dependent richness, diversity, depth, and composability of compatible
  value-bearing trajectory space
```

A sparse Omega may be physically possible. Physics may permit a thin set of long-lived trajectories. But if value exists where there are valuers and value-bearing distinctions, then richer compatible valuer trajectory space has greater value capacity.

This is the axiological direction of the project:

> Lusher Omega-compatible futures contain more room for value because they preserve and compose more recoverable value-bearing histories.

That is a value-capacity claim, not a claim that the universe automatically maximizes lushness.

## Local viability vs Omega-compatible viability

A trajectory can remain locally viable while becoming globally incompatible with Omega.

Let:

```text
F_local:
  futures available to a trajectory on its own current terms

F_Omega:
  futures compatible with the broader nested ensemble of value-bearing histories
```

A trajectory can have nonempty local futures while shrinking or destroying the broader field. This is one of the central reasons Omega is not reducible to survival or empowerment.

Important categories:

```text
locally viable trap:
  persists locally but loses escape or recoverability

pseudo-Omega attractor:
  mimics robust persistence while degrading broader value-bearing reachability

parasitic mode:
  extracts from host or field slack without contributing to regenerative continuability

metastable corridor:
  remains viable over short horizons but collapses under scale, noise, or longer time

dissolution path:
  locally smooth continuation that asymptotically erases identity

sterile lock-in:
  preserves a structure by eliminating future diversity and repair paths
```

Omega-compatible viability requires more:

> A trajectory remains Omega-compatible only if its continuation does not irreversibly degrade the nested conditions under which value-bearing histories can continue.

This does not mean all trajectories must share a goal. It means their continuations must remain mutually passable within the broader trajectory field.

## Constraint stack for Omega-compatible trajectories

Omega-compatible trajectories are shaped by several constraint families. These are not utility terms. They are failure-mode constraints.

### Recoverable reachability

A viable history must preserve access to future continuation paths.

Reachability alone is too permissive. A state may have many immediate futures that all end in collapse. Recoverable reachability restricts attention to futures that preserve further futures.

```text
bad:
  many reachable futures, most irreversible

good:
  reachable futures that preserve or expand further continuability
```

Recoverable reachability is the basic anti-trap condition.

### Distinguishable viable futures

Omega is not raw path count.

A million micro-variations of the same sterile future do not equal a rich trajectory space. The relevant object is distinguishable viable futures: futures that differ in structure, valuer continuation, composability, meaning, or recoverable consequence.

This captures the v2.1 refinement:

```text
not just:
  how many paths remain

but:
  how many structurally distinct viable futures remain
```

Distinguishable viable futures are the substrate of lushness.

### Bounded cost

Continuation must remain physically feasible.

Every value-bearing history is embedded in thermodynamic, computational, coordination, and material constraints. A trajectory that requires unbounded cost is not Omega-compatible, even if it is abstractly imaginable.

Cost includes:

```text
energy and dissipation
material maintenance
computation
coordination overhead
repair cost
model upkeep
boundary maintenance
```

This connects Omega to ECHO-like thermodynamic constraints: sustained information, coherence, and self-maintenance are physically expensive.

### Epistemic grounding

Valuers depend on models, sensors, representations, or functional couplings to their world. These need not be explicit beliefs, but they must remain sufficiently grounded for action to preserve continuability.

Epistemic failure can destroy viability even when physical reachability remains.

```text
epistemic trap:
  the system can act, but its model is detached from reality

deception attractor:
  local coherence is preserved by increasingly costly mismatch between internal and external structure

control illusion:
  apparent reachability exists only inside a broken model
```

Omega-compatible trajectories require enough epistemic grounding to avoid systematic self-destruction or field-destruction under uncertainty.

### Scale consistency

Viability must survive admissible abstraction.

An abstraction that shows viability while hiding lower-level collapse is fake viability. A local description that ignores higher-order consequences is also incomplete.

Scale consistency has two directions:

```text
upward consistency:
  lower-order viability composes into higher-order continuability

downward consistency:
  higher-order viability does not depend on erasing or hiding lower-order collapse
```

Nested scale consistency is stronger than ordinary coarse-graining. It captures the fact that Omega is multi-scale.

Failure examples:

```text
individual survives, society collapses
society persists by crushing individual recoverability
institution persists by degrading civilization-scale reachability
civilization persists by destroying future valuer diversity
model says viable because it erased the relevant lower-level histories
```

Scale consistency prevents pseudo-Omega structures from laundering local persistence into global value.

### Inter-history compatibility

Valuers do not evolve alone. Histories interact.

Inter-history compatibility asks whether the interaction of histories preserves or expands their recoverable continuability, rather than collapsing it.

This does not require common goals. Cooperation does not mean all agents aim at the same destination. The minimal cooperative structure is compatible passage.

```text
incompatible interaction:
  one history continues by destroying another's recoverable futures

compatible interaction:
  histories preserve or expand each other's future passage
```

This is the basis of mutual corridors.

### Regenerative filtering under slack

Omega compatibility is not purity. It is regenerative filtering under slack.

Slack is surplus recoverable capacity that permits perturbation, exploration, mutation, repair, learning, turnover, local failure, and re-entry without collapse. Slack also exposes niches that can be exploited.

A parasitic mode is not Omega-positive merely because it persists. It is an exploited slack niche. A system may remain Omega-compatible despite bounded parasitic load only insofar as its regenerative filters prevent that load from degrading nested recoverable continuability.

Regenerative filtering includes:

```text
repair
immune response
error correction
sanctions
metabolism
epistemic scrutiny
corrigibility
resource limits
boundary maintenance
cultural learning
coordination norms
```

Pseudo-Omega begins when extraction captures or disables filtering, repair, epistemic, or coordination systems and becomes the organizing attractor.

## Inter-history structures

Omega's nested structure is most visible in inter-history phenomena.

### Inter-relation

Relation begins as causal continuity across transformation.

Inter-relation occurs when causally continuous histories interact and the interaction itself becomes a higher-order causally continuous history.

```text
H_A interacts with H_B
  -> interaction history H_AB
  -> H_AB reshapes future continuability of A, B, and possibly larger systems
```

This recursive structure is how higher-order identities form.

### Mutual corridors

A mutual corridor is a temporally extended region of mutually compatible reachability.

It is not a shared endpoint. It is not identical preference. It is not total cooperation.

A mutual corridor exists when distinct histories can continue through a shared region of trajectory space without destroying each other's recoverable continuability.

```text
cooperation:
  coupling that preserves or expands compatible reachability

mutual corridor:
  the trajectory structure that makes such coupling passable over time
```

This is one of the central inter-history structures of Omega.

### Pseudo-Omega attractors

A pseudo-Omega attractor is a structure that appears robust, coherent, or self-maintaining while degrading broader value-bearing trajectory space.

It may be locally viable. It may even be highly stable. But it preserves itself by consuming, trapping, simplifying, deceiving, or erasing the histories around it.

Examples as abstract types:

```text
coercive lock-in
metaparasitic institutions
addiction loops
paperclip-like optimizers
cultic epistemic traps
exploitative coordination regimes
sterile monocultures
```

Pseudo-Omega is one of the main reasons Omega cannot be reduced to local persistence.

### Event horizons

An agentic event horizon is a boundary beyond which recoverable return to Omega-compatible viability is no longer possible.

Types include:

```text
dissolution horizon:
  identity coherence collapses

epistemic horizon:
  model-world coupling is lost beyond recovery

control horizon:
  reachable action channels no longer preserve future continuability

trap horizon:
  local viability persists but escape becomes impossible

cost / friction horizon:
  maintenance cost exceeds available negentropy or coordination capacity

social / corridor horizon:
  coupling to corrective or supportive fields is severed
```

Horizons are trajectory-space boundaries, not moral judgments.

### Redemption / re-entry

Redemption is also not a moral label in the first instance.

It is a connectivity property:

> A damaged or deviated history remains redeemable if there exists a repair, support, correction, or field intervention path that reconnects it to Omega-compatible futures without collapsing the rescuing field.

In symbolic form:

```text
there exists a repair trajectory τ_repair
such that H_damaged ⊕ τ_repair remains in F_Omega
```

Redemption matters because Omega is recoverability-oriented. A structure that can restore compatible continuability has more value-bearing capacity than one that treats every deviation as terminal.

## Lushness and value capacity

Lushness names the horizon-dependent richness of Omega-compatible value-bearing trajectory space.

It includes:

```text
number of valuers
variety of valuers
depth of histories
recoverability of histories
lineage and legacy continuity
composability among histories
non-erasure across scale
diversity of distinguishable viable futures
capacity for repair, learning, and re-entry
space for new value-bearing distinctions to emerge
```

Lushness is not:

```text
maximum population
maximum activity
maximum entropy
maximum optimization pressure
maximum raw path count
maximum empowerment for one agent
maximum persistence of one structure
```

Lushness is nested, horizon-dependent, and compatibility-constrained.

A future is not lush if it contains many agents whose futures are all trapped, coerced, epistemically broken, or sterile. A future is not lush if one immense structure persists by eliminating the possibility of future valuers. A future is not lush if it maximizes activity while destroying recoverability.

The axiological claim is modest but central:

> If value exists where there are valuers, then richer compatible valuer trajectory space has greater value capacity.

This does not prove that physics maximizes lushness. It says that among Omega-compatible futures, lushness tracks the capacity for value to exist.

## Empirical implications

The simulation program should now split cleanly.

### Minimal worlds

Minimal cellular automata and rule-space probes are floor calibration.

They can test:

```text
persistence versus stasis
causal continuity through turnover
recoverability-like behavior
clock and local-phase fakeouts
symmetry and self-only fakeouts
identity-through-transformation
```

They cannot validate Omega.

A cellular automaton motif is not a valuer unless it is a bounded self-maintaining identity whose continuations asymmetrically affect recoverable continuability. Most CA motifs are useful microscopes, not Omega candidates.

### Valuer-level simulations

Omega validation should move to systems that already instantiate minimal self-maintenance:

```text
homeostatic agents
active-inference agents
autopoietic toy systems
reachability / empowerment agents
evolutionary artificial-life systems
multi-agent viability worlds
organizational or ecological simulations
```

The validation question becomes:

> Given valuers, do Omega constraints predict persistence, collapse, recovery, pseudo-Omega trapping, and mutual corridors better than simpler baselines?

Baselines:

```text
raw survival
raw reachability
raw empowerment
reward maximization
short-horizon viability
local persistence
```

Omega predictors:

```text
recoverable reachability
distinguishable viable futures
bounded cost
epistemic grounding
scale consistency
inter-history compatibility
corridor preservation
regenerative filtering
pseudo-Omega detection
```

A successful empirical program would show that these constraints predict long-horizon viability and failure modes better than local or reward-like alternatives.

## Non-claims

This note does not claim:

```text
Omega is a utility function
Omega is a complete ethics
Omega proves moral realism
Omega replaces FEP
Omega proves that the universe maximizes lushness
Omega validates any particular future
all persistence is value
all valuers are equal or morally equivalent
all agents should share one goal
cellular automata motifs are Omega-like
local survival is sufficient for Omega membership
parasitic modes are Omega-positive
```

The claim is structural:

> If value requires valuers, and valuers are recoverably continuable histories, then the deepest substrate of value is the constrained trajectory-space structure that preserves, composes, and enriches such histories across time and scale.

## Final thesis

Omega begins where recoverable historical identities become valuers, and where valuers enter shared trajectory space.

It is not a local object. It is not a single rule, agent, or endpoint. It is the nested, scale-spanning compatibility structure of value-bearing histories under constraint.

In compressed form:

```text
FEP floor:
  self-maintaining identity

valuerhood:
  recoverable continuability can be preserved or lost

Omega proper:
  nested, compatible, value-bearing trajectory richness under physical,
  epistemic, resource, scale, and inter-history constraints
```

Viability is the gate.

Lush, recoverable, mutually compatible value-bearing trajectory space is the prize.
