# Omega Glossary

Canonical working definitions for the current theory stack

## Purpose

This glossary anchors the current terminology used across the Omega research notes.

It is a working glossary, not a final formal system. Its purpose is to reduce definitional drift as the project moves between primitive theory, FEP/self-maintenance, valuerhood, Omega trajectory space, and empirical validation.

Companion glossaries:

```text
public_terms_and_translations.md:
  public-facing translations for internal terms

historical_probe_terms.md:
  older COM/fiber, trajectory-space, DA/DAR/DAX, and probe-result terminology
```

The guiding dependency chain is:

```text
distinction
  -> asymmetry
  -> relation
  -> identity
  -> recoverability
  -> valuerhood
  -> viability
  -> value-bearing trajectory space
  -> Omega compatibility
```

Lushness is adjacent to this chain rather than identical to Omega. It names structured branching that propagates. Omega-compatible lushness is lushness filtered by recoverable value-bearing compatibility.

The core correction in the current stack is that relation is more primitive than identity. Relation is causal continuity. Identity is organized causal continuity through change.

## Onboarding description

### Structural theory of value-bearing futures

Omega is currently best introduced as a structural theory of value-bearing futures.

The phrase means that Omega studies the structure of possible continuations in which value-bearing histories can remain recoverable, compatible, and richly continuable under constraint. It is meant as a clearer public-facing description than "minimal axiology," while preserving the connection to formal value theory, axiology, and alignment work on future-preserving reachability.

## Primitive layer

### Distinction

Difference can exist.

A distinction is the minimal condition for anything to be discriminable from anything else.

Distinction does not yet imply persistence, identity, value, agency, or meaning. It only says that differences are available for dynamics to act upon.

### Asymmetry

Different paths produce different consequences.

Asymmetry means path non-equivalence. If all paths were consequence-equivalent, there would be no meaningful selection, filtering, learning, loss, recovery, or value-bearing difference.

Asymmetry is not moral hierarchy, deontology, preference, or imposed ranking. It is the fact that different trajectories can lead to different future possibility spaces.

### Relation

Causal continuity links differences across transformation.

Relation is more primitive than identity. It is the causal linkage by which one state, event, or configuration is continuous with another through change.

Relation is not first social relation, graph adjacency, spatial coupling, neighbor dependence, or inter-agent interaction. Those are implementations or higher-order expressions.

A useful image is a vortex. The water molecules churn through it, but the vortex remains related to itself by causal continuity of organization.

### Inter-relation

Inter-relation is relation at a higher order.

Histories interact, and their interaction itself becomes a causally continuous history.

```text
H_A interacts with H_B
  -> interaction history H_AB
```

Inter-relation is not a new primitive. It is recursive relation among histories.

## Identity layer

### Identity

Identity is organized causal continuity through change.

An identity is not fixed material sameness and not exact state recurrence. It is a coherent causal organization that remains continuous through transformation, constituent turnover, or perturbation.

The Ship of Theseus is an identity question because it asks whether the causal organization of the ship remains sufficiently continuous through material replacement.

Identity must avoid two errors:

```text
too broad:
  everything maps to the same identity

too narrow:
  only exact state equality counts
```

A valid identity preserves continuation-relevant causal organization.

### Historical identity

A historical identity is an identity considered as a path or history.

```text
X0 -> X1 -> X2 -> ... -> Xt
```

A sequence becomes a historical identity when causal continuity across the path coheres as the continuation of the same process.

### Identity class

An identity class is an equivalence over causally continuous histories.

It allows transformation while preserving the causal organization needed for continuability.

Identity classes are useful only if they preserve continuation-relevant distinctions. They are invalid if they collapse everything together or if they are so strict that identity cannot survive transformation.

### Continuability

Continuability is the availability of future paths preserving identity-, lineage-, or field-relevant causal organization.

Continuability is not merely the existence of one next state. It is the availability of admissible future histories that preserve or transform the relevant identity without erasing the causal organization needed for further continuation.

Continuability should be indexed to:

```text
identity
horizon
constraints
scale
```

### Recoverability

Recoverability is perturbation-continuability.

After disruption, there remains a path back into, or still within, a continuable identity class.

Recoverability does not require exact restoration.

```text
not merely:
  return to exact prior state

but:
  remain within a continuable identity class
```

Recoverability is the floor separating cheap persistence from proto-value.

## Valuerhood layer

### Valuer

A valuer is a bounded historical identity for which different continuations asymmetrically affect recoverable continuability.

This definition does not require consciousness, explicit preference, utility maximization, reward, or human-like goals.

A valuer requires:

```text
boundary:
  enough distinction between process and environment for identity to be meaningful

causal continuity:
  identity organized through transformation rather than fixed constituents

recoverability:
  perturbations do not immediately erase continuability

path consequence:
  different continuations change future viability

self-maintaining interaction:
  interaction can preserve, degrade, restore, or expand continued identity
```

### Proto-value

Proto-value is the emergence of a locus for which continuation, degradation, recovery, and loss are dynamically non-equivalent.

Proto-value is not preference, morality, or utility. It is the structural asymmetry created when a bounded historical identity can continue, degrade, recover, or collapse.

### Agent

An agent is a valuer with action channels that modulate its future continuability.

Agency is stronger than valuerhood. A valuer need not be reflective, cognitive, or explicitly goal-directed. An agent has some capacity to select, regulate, or modulate trajectories in ways that affect its continuability.

### Reflective or cognitive valuer

A reflective or cognitive valuer is a valuer whose internal dynamics model, represent, or explicitly weight possible futures.

Examples include active inference systems, learning agents, animals, humans, and organizations with self-models.

## Viability layer

### Viability

Viability is constraint-satisfying continuability over a horizon.

Viability is not generic survival. It is indexed to identity, lineage, or field.

```text
direct viability:
  an identity itself remains continuable

recoverable viability:
  perturbations do not erase access to the identity's continuable class

lineage viability:
  successor histories preserve enough causal organization or value-bearing capacity

field viability:
  the broader ecology or trajectory field remains capable of generating, sustaining,
  repairing, and composing value-bearing histories
```

### Local viability

Local viability is continuation on a trajectory's own current terms.

A locally viable trajectory may still be Omega-incompatible if it degrades the broader field of value-bearing histories.

### Omega-compatible viability

Omega-compatible viability is value-bearing viability compatible with the broader nested field of recoverable value-bearing histories.

A trajectory is Omega-compatible only if its continuation does not irreversibly degrade the nested conditions under which value-bearing histories can continue.

### Legacy / lineage continuity

Legacy is continuity through successor histories. The cleaner formal term is lineage continuity.

Lineage continuity preserves or transmits causal organization, value-bearing capacity, or recoverable continuability into successors.

Legacy is not mere memory, influence, or trace. It is relevant when it preserves continuation-relevant structure such as knowledge, repair capacity, institutions, norms, lineages, ecological roles, or corridors.

### Churn

Churn is turnover inside a larger continuity.

Churn can be compatible with Omega when it supports higher-order recoverable continuability.

```text
regenerative churn:
  lower-order turnover supports higher-order continuability

destructive churn:
  turnover erases the causal organization required for continuability
```

Examples include cell turnover in organisms, birth/death in ecologies, and generational succession in civilizations.

## Omega layer

### Value-bearing

A history is value-bearing when continuation, degradation, recovery, and loss are dynamically non-equivalent for a valuer.

Value-bearing is local to a valuer or valuer-history. It is not yet the same as Omega-compatible.

A parasite or trap may be locally value-bearing for itself while being Omega-incompatible globally.

### Omega

Omega is the nested, scale-spanning compatibility structure of value-bearing histories and inter-histories that remain recoverably continuable under physical, epistemic, resource, scale, and inter-history constraints.

Omega is not a utility function, not a single endpoint, and not mere survival.

### Alpha-Omega

Alpha-Omega is the full arc of value-bearing trajectory space from emergence to asymptotic persistence.

```text
Alpha:
  emergence of distinction-preserving, recoverably continuable valuers

Omega:
  asymptotic compatibility structure of value-bearing trajectories

Alpha-Omega:
  nested trajectory geometry from emergence to long-horizon compatible persistence
```

### Proto-Omega

Proto-Omega is an early precursor of Omega: reachable possibility that remains future-bearing across time.

In VAL0-CT, proto-Omega is operationalized as future-bearing reachability in constructor-style task algebras. It is not full Omega, not valuerhood, and not a moral claim.

### Omega-compatible

A history is Omega-compatible if its value-bearing continuation remains compatible with the nested recoverable continuability of broader value-bearing trajectory space.

Omega compatibility must be indexed to horizon, scale, and constraint set.

### Lushness

Lushness is structured branching that propagates.

A future possibility space is lush when its branches are distinguishable, non-noise, and carry forward enough structure to enable further meaningful branching.

Lushness is more primitive than Omega compatibility. Non-Omega trajectories can be lush: a runaway optimizer, cancerous growth, or destructive institution may generate structured propagating branches while degrading broader recoverable possibility.

```text
lushness:
  structured branching that propagates

Omega-compatible lushness:
  structured branching that remains compatible with recoverable value-bearing continuability
```

Lushness is not:

```text
maximum population
maximum activity
maximum entropy
maximum raw path count
maximum optimization pressure
maximum empowerment for one agent
maximum persistence of one structure
mere noise branching
```

## Constraint and failure-mode layer

### Recoverable reachability

Recoverable reachability is access to future continuation paths that preserve further continuability.

Reachability alone is too permissive. A state may have many immediate futures that all end in collapse.

### Distinguishable viable futures

Distinguishable viable futures are structurally distinct futures that preserve value-bearing continuability.

They are not merely micro-variations of the same sterile future.

### Bounded cost

Bounded cost means continuability remains physically, computationally, materially, and coordinatively feasible.

Cost includes energy, dissipation, material upkeep, computation, repair, model maintenance, boundary maintenance, and coordination overhead.

### Epistemic grounding

Epistemic grounding means models, sensors, representations, or functional couplings remain sufficiently connected to reality to support continuability.

Epistemic failure can destroy viability even when physical reachability remains.

### Scale consistency

Scale consistency means viability claims survive admissible abstraction.

Abstraction must neither invent viability nor hide collapse.

```text
upward consistency:
  lower-order viability composes into higher-order continuability

downward consistency:
  higher-order viability does not depend on erasing or hiding lower-order collapse
```

### Inter-history compatibility

Inter-history compatibility means interacting histories preserve or expand recoverable continuability rather than collapsing it.

This does not require shared goals. It requires compatible passage.

### Mutual corridor

A mutual corridor is a temporally extended region of mutually compatible reachability.

A mutual corridor allows distinct histories to continue through shared trajectory space without destroying each other's recoverable continuability.

### Slack

Slack is surplus recoverable capacity that permits perturbation, exploration, mutation, repair, learning, turnover, local failure, and re-entry without collapse.

Slack is necessary for robustness and adaptation, but it also exposes niches that can be exploited.

### Regenerative filtering

Regenerative filtering is the field's capacity to prevent destructive modes from becoming organizing attractors while preserving enough slack for adaptation.

Filtering can include repair, immune response, error correction, sanctions, metabolism, epistemic scrutiny, corrigibility, resource limits, boundary maintenance, cultural learning, and coordination norms.

### Parasitic mode

A parasitic mode is a trajectory pattern that extracts continuability from a host or field without contributing to regenerative continuability.

Parasitism is not Omega-positive. It is an exploited slack niche.

A system may remain Omega-compatible despite bounded parasitic load only insofar as regenerative filters prevent that load from degrading nested recoverable continuability.

### Pseudo-Omega / destructive lock-in

Pseudo-Omega is destructive lock-in: local success that collapses broader future possibility.

A pseudo-Omega structure looks persistent, robust, optimized, or locally viable, but it preserves or expands itself by degrading nested recoverable value-bearing trajectory space.

Pseudo-Omega begins when destructive modes capture or disable repair, filtering, epistemic, coordination, or regenerative systems and become organizing attractors.

Plain-language examples:

```text
proxy optimization that destroys the real target
self-preservation that removes correction or repair paths
local control that collapses broader future options
institutional persistence that destroys member recoverability
lock-in that makes better futures unreachable
```

### Event horizon

An event horizon is a trajectory-space boundary beyond which recoverable return to Omega-compatible viability is no longer possible.

Types include dissolution horizons, epistemic horizons, control horizons, trap horizons, cost/friction horizons, and social/corridor horizons.

### Redemption / re-entry

Redemption or re-entry is trajectory connectivity back into Omega-compatible futures.

It is not first a moral label. It means a damaged or deviated history remains reconnectable to Omega-compatible futures through repair, support, correction, or field intervention.

## Fakeout layer

### Fakeout

A fakeout is apparent satisfaction of a higher-level claim by a lower-level structure.

A fakeout is not necessarily false, useless, or non-Omega at every scale. It is insufficient evidence for the claim currently being made.

Use:

```text
fakeout_for(claim_level)
```

Examples:

```text
clock:
  fakeout for recoverable historical identity
  but valid component of metabolism, timing, or regulation

stasis:
  fakeout for self-maintaining agency
  but valid component of memory, boundary, or scaffold

self-copy:
  fakeout for full relational identity
  but possible component of lineage viability

local phase:
  fakeout for historical identity
  but possible proto-memory or regulatory component

parasitic persistence:
  fakeout for Omega-compatible mutuality
  but evidence of an exposed slack niche
```

### Cheap factorization

Cheap factorization is lower-complexity explanatory compression that preserves an apparent score while losing the target structure.

Examples:

```text
stasis:
  identity compresses to one state

clock:
  identity compresses to phase

noise:
  identity compresses to distribution

lock-in:
  viability compresses to terminal attractor

symmetry:
  path consequence erased by quotient

self-only:
  relation collapses to isolated recurrence
```

Cheap means formally compressive, not aesthetically unimpressive.

## Anti-drift principles

### Identity must not be arbitrary

No identity relation is valid merely because it can be defined.

It is valid only if it preserves continuation-relevant causal organization and does not collapse into overbroad or underbroad equivalence.

### Viability must be indexed

Viability claims should specify identity, lineage, or field; horizon; constraints; and scale.

### Value-bearing is not Omega-compatible by default

A locally value-bearing history may be Omega-incompatible if it degrades broader recoverable value-bearing trajectory space.

### Omega compatibility is not purity

Omega compatibility allows churn, failure, competition, error, and bounded parasitic load when regenerative filtering prevents degradation of the broader field.

### Parasitism is not Omega-positive

A system may remain Omega-compatible despite parasitic load. The compatible structure is regenerative filtering, not parasitism.

### Fakeouts are level-indexed

A lower-order structure may be a fakeout for a higher-level claim while remaining a valid component at its own level.

### Lushness is not Omega by itself

Lushness names structured propagation of branching possibilities. Omega compatibility filters that lushness for recoverable value-bearing continuability.
