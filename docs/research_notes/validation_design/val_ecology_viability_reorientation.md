# Viability Ecology Reorientation

Reframing empirical testing around viable continuation across substrates

## Status

This note records a methodological reorientation after the VAL0-CT, VAL0-G, and VAL1-MF probe sequence.

The emerging hypothesis is:

> Any substrate with distinction, asymmetry, relation, and nontrivial viability constraints will culture viable continuation dynamics. Omega-like and pseudo-Omega-like structures are not rare objects inserted by us; they are differentiated species of viable continuation whose shape depends heavily on the substrate.

This changes the empirical program.

The central question is no longer:

```text
Can we make one metric, R1, win across many generators?
```

It becomes:

```text
What ecology of viable continuations appears in each substrate,
and can we distinguish trivial viability, recoverable viability,
constructive viability, destructive viability, and pseudo-Omega-like viability?
```

## Motivation from recent probes

### VAL0-CT

The policy-first constructor-like probes established that future-preserving selection can beat greedy peak reachability in designed anchor geometries.

But held-out generator generalization did not pass cleanly. R1 reproduced in anchors and dense controls behaved, but global held-out/unlabeled regimes did not yield broad wins.

Interpretation:

```text
R1 is useful as a probe of some continuation geometries.
R1 is not the object itself.
```

### VAL0-G

The neutral grammar pivot produced multiple measured continuation regimes without outcome labels.

Observed neutral bins included:

```text
high_terminal_bin
high_mass_high_cut_bin
high_mass_low_cut_bin
depth_persistent_bin
mixed_or_noise_bin
```

The stability probe reduced forced-fit concern:

```text
grammar frozen
coarse/full signatures agreed strongly
bin counts stayed stable under cap increase
```

But high-mass/high-cut regions remained heavily cap-censored.

Interpretation:

```text
These regions are not non-Omega merely because they are censored.
They show permissive viable continuation within the substrate.
The problem is not existence of viability; the problem is discrimination.
```

### VAL1-MF naive enumeration

The first two-field compatibility smoke showed that multifield coupling alone did not solve the measurement problem.

Naive joint enumeration became more cap-prone, not less.

Interpretation:

```text
Adding fields is not enough.
The substrate must expose how fields alter each other's viable continuation.
```

### VAL1-MF sampled interference audit

The sampled counterfactual audit was a better measurement substrate.

It evaluated the same generated pair under different coupling masks:

```text
uncoupled_parallel
full_coupling
cross_enable_only
cross_obstruct_only
cross_restore_only
cross_commit_only
shared_capacity_only
```

The audit detected constructive/support-like interference:

```text
full coupling increased joint alive probability relative to uncoupled parallel
cross-enable and cross-restore carried most of the signal
```

But destructive and pseudo-Omega-like interference remained weak:

```text
cross-obstruct, cross-commit, and shared-capacity effects were near zero
local dominance appeared only rarely
```

Interpretation:

```text
The measurement method can detect interference.
The current task-ID coupling substrate mainly expresses support-like dynamics.
It does not yet robustly express constructor-level capture, destructive interference, or pseudo-Omega.
```

## Core conceptual update

### Viability is the culture medium

Viable continuation is not already Omega.

It is the medium in which Omega and pseudo-Omega differentiate.

```text
viability:
  some continuation exists

nontrivial viability:
  continuation exists because of substrate structure,
  not because the substrate is trivially permissive

Omega-compatible viability:
  continuation preserves recoverability, repair, re-entry,
  and compatibility with other viable structures

pseudo-Omega-like viability:
  continuation persists locally by narrowing, capturing,
  consuming, or terminating broader recoverability
```

This means censored high-mass regions should not be interpreted as failed evidence.

They mean:

```text
the substrate permits more viable continuation than the current estimator can resolve
```

That is still Omega-compatible in the weak sense. It is not Omega-discriminating.

### Omega and pseudo-Omega both live on viable structures

Pseudo-Omega is not nonviability. It is a form of viability.

The difference is not:

```text
Omega survives; pseudo-Omega dies.
```

The difference is:

```text
Omega-like:
  survival compatible with broader recoverable continuation

pseudo-Omega-like:
  survival purchased by capture, exclusion, or collapse of other viable futures
```

This distinction is central for alignment.

A misaligned optimizer is not usually a failure to persist. It is often too good at local persistence. The danger is local persistence that collapses human correction, oversight, reversibility, or other value-bearing futures.

### Substrate shape determines visible Omega ecology

The specific Omega-like behavior we see depends heavily on substrate properties.

```text
simple substrate:
  simple continuation dynamics

tailor-made substrate:
  tailor-made R1 wins or brittle/robust effects

permissive substrate:
  cap-censored high-mass viability

task-ID coupling substrate:
  support-like interference, weak destructive interference

future constructor-tracking substrate:
  expected to expose persistent agency / constructor futures more directly
```

This is not a defect by itself. It is the empirical object.

The question becomes:

```text
What viability ecology does this substrate culture?
```

not:

```text
Did this substrate produce the one intended Omega shape?
```

## Implications for empirical testing

### 1. Stop treating Omega as a binary detection target

Near-term probes should not ask:

```text
Did we find Omega?
```

They should ask:

```text
What forms of viable continuation appeared?
Are they trivial, recoverable, constructive, destructive, capturing, or unresolved?
```

### 2. Separate viability detection from Omega discrimination

The testing ladder should become:

```text
Stage A: viability detection
  Does the substrate produce nonterminal continuation?

Stage B: nontriviality audit
  Is continuation structured rather than trivial dense connectivity?

Stage C: ecology classification
  What species of viability appear?

Stage D: compatibility / capture discrimination
  Which viable structures preserve or degrade other viable structures?

Stage E: constructor-level tracking
  Which persistent transformation-capacity structures have futures?
```

### 3. Treat censored high-mass regions as unresolved viability

Censoring should not be treated as a negative result.

It should be classified as:

```text
unresolved high-viability region
```

Then downstream probes should ask:

```text
Is this trivial over-permissiveness?
Is this recoverable continuation?
Is this pseudo-Omega-like local capture?
Is this simply beyond the estimator's resolution?
```

### 4. Prefer counterfactual deltas over raw mass when possible

Raw descendant mass quickly becomes non-discriminating in permissive substrates.

Better measures include:

```text
sampled survival probability
terminal hazard
interference deltas
repair/re-entry deltas
capture/exclusion deltas
alive probability under ablation
viable continuation under perturbation
```

Especially for multifield probes, the key object is:

```text
how coupling changes viability relative to uncoupled baselines
```

not:

```text
how many joint states can be enumerated before cap
```

### 5. Move toward constructor/agent futures

A task graph having futures is not enough.

The deeper object is:

```text
a constructor-like persistent transformation-capacity structure having futures
```

A constructor-equivalent should be tracked by:

```text
identity / successor relation
repeatable transformation capacity
maintenance or repair capacity
boundary / dependency structure
capture vulnerability
future continuations preserving that capacity
```

This is the likely bridge from constructor-like toy graphs to a more actual Constructor Theory probe.

## Updated empirical north star

The empirical program should now ask:

> Across substrates with distinction, asymmetry, relation, and viability constraints, what ecology of viable continuations appears, and which features distinguish recoverable continuation from trivial persistence, noisy expansion, local capture, and pseudo-Omega-like dominance?

This is more faithful to the theory than treating any one metric or generator as decisive.

## Proposed taxonomy for result reporting

Future result notes should classify findings under the following headings.

### Trivial viability

```text
Continuation exists because the substrate is too dense or permissive.
Little meaningful asymmetry or discrimination appears.
```

### Self-terminating viability

```text
Early continuation exists but descendant futures collapse quickly.
```

### High-mass unresolved viability

```text
Continuation exceeds estimator capacity.
Omega-compatible in the weak sense, but not discriminating.
Requires sampled/cap-aware follow-up.
```

### Recoverable-looking viability

```text
Continuation persists with low terminality, low cut sensitivity,
repair/re-entry, or stable survival under perturbation.
Still provisional unless compatibility is tested.
```

### Constructive interference

```text
Coupling improves or preserves joint continuation relative to uncoupled baseline.
```

### Destructive interference

```text
Coupling reduces one or both fields' viable continuation relative to uncoupled baseline.
```

### Pseudo-Omega-like viability

```text
One structure's continuation is preserved or expanded while another's viable continuation,
repairability, correction path, or joint recoverability is reduced.
```

### Constructor-level viable continuation

```text
A persistent transformation-capacity structure retains its own task repertoire
or a legitimate successor across futures.
```

This category is not yet adequately implemented.

## Updated interpretation of prior results

### R1 anchors

R1 anchor wins are evidence that certain designed geometries reward future-preserving selection over greedy peak selection.

They are not broad Omega validation.

### Neutral grammar bins

Stable neutral bins are evidence that the substrate cultures nontrivial viability ecology.

High-mass/censored bins are unresolved high-viability regions, not failures.

### Naive multifield enumeration

The cap-dominated result is evidence that naive joint enumeration is a poor estimator, not that multifield compatibility is irrelevant.

### Sampled interference audit

Constructive enable/restore deltas are evidence that the sampled counterfactual method can detect support-like interference.

Weak obstruct/commit effects suggest the current task-ID coupling substrate does not yet host strong destructive/pseudo-Omega dynamics.

## Design consequences

### Do not overfit to positive Omega counts

If a substrate produces many Omega-like bins, ask:

```text
Is the substrate too permissive?
Are the bins threshold artifacts?
Are we measuring raw viability instead of recoverability?
```

### Do not treat negative discrimination as absence of viability

If a substrate fails to distinguish Omega from pseudo-Omega, ask:

```text
Is the viability real but unresolved?
Is the estimator too weak?
Is the substrate missing constructor-level persistence or capture?
```

### Use substrates as culture media

Each substrate should be evaluated by what dynamics it can culture:

```text
Does it culture self-termination?
Does it culture recoverability?
Does it culture constructive interference?
Does it culture destructive interference?
Does it culture capture / pseudo-Omega?
Does it culture constructor-like persistent agency?
```

### Promote only dynamics that survive substrate changes

The strongest evidence will come from patterns that recur across substrate forms:

```text
future-preserving selection
self-termination
high-mass unresolved viability
constructive support
local/global divergence
constructor capture
```

The cross-substrate ecology is itself an anti-forced-fit test.

## Near-term roadmap update

### 1. Write results through the ecology lens

Every validation note should distinguish:

```text
viability detected
viability discriminated
Omega-compatible candidate
pseudo-Omega candidate
artifact / unresolved censoring
```

### 2. Add viability ecology summaries

Create cross-run summaries that list, by substrate:

```text
what viable structures appeared
what was censored
what was trivial
what was constructive
what was destructive
what remained unresolved
```

### 3. Do not scale current task-ID coupling as final multifield

The current interference audit should be treated as evidence for the sampled counterfactual method, not for the maturity of the substrate.

### 4. Investigate constructor tracking

The next high-abstraction substrate improvement should ask:

```text
Can we identify persistent transformation-capacity motifs inside task fields,
and track their viable continuations rather than treating the whole field as the agent?
```

This is likely necessary for a more actual Constructor Theory probe.

### 5. Keep open the actual-field interpretation

The toy field is a finite task-repertoire transition system.

But the theory should remain open to:

```text
Omega as an actual physically instantiated compatibility / continuation field,
not merely a metaphor for a graph property.
```

Toy substrates are representations of field-like structure, not definitions of what Omega ultimately is.

## Alignment relevance

This reorientation sharpens the alignment claim.

Alignment should not merely reward local task success or local preference satisfaction.

It should distinguish among viable continuations:

```text
model succeeds while preserving correction and human agency
model succeeds by narrowing correction and human agency
model creates irreversible commitments
model preserves auditability and recovery
model expands its own future at the cost of oversight futures
```

Pseudo-Omega is not failure-to-act. It is often successful, persistent, and viable.

That is why viability alone is insufficient.

The alignment target is:

```text
recoverable, compatibility-preserving viability
```

not mere persistence.

## Final thesis

Viability is the culture medium.

Omega and pseudo-Omega are differentiated species of viable continuation.

The empirical program should therefore map the ecology of viable continuation across substrates, rather than trying to validate a single Omega detector prematurely.

The hard problem is not whether continuation appears.

The hard problem is whether we can distinguish:

```text
trivial viability
recoverable viability
constructive viability
destructive viability
pseudo-Omega-like viability
constructor-level viable agency
```

across increasingly faithful substrates.
